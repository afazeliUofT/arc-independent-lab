## 1. Drescher's schema mechanism: representation construction, not just retention

**Original methods.** The system starts with supplied sensory items, motor actions, goals and a deliberately designed microworld; its representations are not learned from raw vision. Context–action–result schemas expand through statistics linking actions to outcomes and outcome reliability to context. Synthetic items name unobserved conditions governing a schema's success. The paper describes several ways of maintaining them, including consequences of other reliable schemas. The reported implementation is explicitly partial: synthetic items and composite actions are still absent. Drescher documents slowing with growing schema count and the cost of schema–item cross-connectivity. This is contemporary evidence of a computational obstacle, not evidence that the programme was abandoned for that reason. **2026 inference:** more hardware can raise a fixed implementation's practical ceiling; it does not remove growth in schema–item associations or discover which missing contexts matter. Neither practical scaling nor an expired hardware bottleneck has been measured here. [Drescher, 1987, *A Mechanism for Early Piagetian Learning*, AAAI proceedings, published primary conference paper, pp. 290–294; complete substantive text including methods and implementation read](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1987/AAAI87-052.pdf). Accessed 2026-09-06.

**Later primary test and a discrepancy to resolve.** Holmes and Isbell compare modified and original schema learners in small partially observed systems with randomly selected actions. They attribute an original learner's poor prediction partly to retaining transient synthetic state according to duration. However, their account of original maintenance as duration-based does not reconcile with Drescher's earlier explicit prediction-based maintenance clause. The comparison may concern a particular implementation or later specification; the missing book/thesis must decide. The larger speech demonstration disables synthetic items, restricts context size and removes weak schemas for tractability. It therefore does not establish scalable hidden-state construction. These are informative restrictions, not a demonstrated general failure of the constructivist premise. [Holmes and Isbell, 2004, *Schema Learning: Experience-Based Construction of Predictive Action Models*, NeurIPS proceedings, published primary conference paper, §§2–4 and conclusions inspected](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2004/file/18bb68e2b38e4a8ce7cf4f6b2625768c-Paper.pdf). Accessed 2026-09-06.

**Abandonment status:** unresolved for Drescher's particular implementation, false as a blanket description of schema-based research. A later implemented robot-controller study establishes continuation, without establishing equivalence to Drescher or success on this programme's entire demand. [Lagriffoul and Tstotsos, 2022, *A Schema-Based Robot Controller Complying With the Constraints of Biological Systems*, published primary article; existence/lineage used here, not efficacy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9124795/). Accessed 2026-09-06.

## 2. Explanation-based learning and expensive chunks: retained knowledge can make action worse

**Computational demand and supplied knowledge.** PRODIGY's explanation-based learner turns a problem-solving trace into more directly usable control knowledge. It receives a domain theory, operators, target concepts, and an operationality criterion; it is not discovering the world's laws from scratch. Minton's methods estimate a rule's usefulness from expected savings, applicability and matching cost, then validate that estimate during subsequent use. Removing that validation worsens performance; the evaluation compares learned, hand-written and absent control rules. Test-phase timings omit much of the learning expense, and the paper points to the dissertation for generator detail. Its contemporary objection to a hardware-only answer is specific: arbitrarily expressive, growing descriptions retain hard matching cases. **2026 inference:** a faster machine changes constants and feasible scale, not that worst-case result. This does not establish typical-case difficulty in present systems. [Minton, 1988, *Quantitative Results Concerning the Utility of Explanation-Based Learning*, AAAI proceedings, published primary conference paper, §§2–5 methods and ablation text read](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1988/AAAI88-100.pdf). Accessed 2026-09-06.

**Mechanistic evidence.** Tambe, Newell and Rosenbloom instrument production matching as a search over partial variable bindings. Expensive chunks arise chiefly from branching matches and ordering, rather than merely long rules. An optimal-ordering experiment reduces part of the observed overhead but leaves the representational source. Their restricted representation trades generality for cheaper matching. This is a traced failure of applying retained rules; it must be separated from forgetting and from adding operators that increase the planner's action branching. Methods assume symbolic problem spaces, working-memory descriptions and existing operators. **2026 inference:** compute can relieve measured matching overhead on bounded tasks; preserving unrestricted relational expressiveness leaves the underlying combinatorial possibility. No cross-era runtime comparison was performed. [Tambe, Newell and Rosenbloom, 1990, *The Problem of Expensive Chunks and Its Solution by Restricting Expressiveness*, *Machine Learning*, published primary article; §§3–5 and §8.4 inspected](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/BF00117107.pdf). Accessed 2026-09-06.

**Abandonment status:** these papers establish obstacles, not the cause of any community-wide decline. Soar's official site still distributes its architecture and documents chunking, semantic memory and episodic memory. Continuation of Soar does not establish that every historical EBL system survives. [Soar project, current official homepage and manual links; primary software documentation](https://soar.eecs.umich.edu/). Accessed 2026-09-06.

## 3. Adaptive resonance: stability is conditional on the representation and learning rules

**Demand and assumptions.** ART's original mathematical target is stable category learning from sequential binary patterns. It assumes a supplied input feature space, category units, vigilance, constrained initial weights and learning timescales. Its matching/reset process checks whether an active category adequately represents an input before changing the associated long-term traces. The proof conditions include resetting short-term activity between learning trials. Long-term category stability therefore already spans one specific reset; it is not evidence of causal-world discovery, arbitrary recombination or appropriate preservation after a representation change. **2026 inference:** the scientific restriction is the task and representation covered by the theorem. Faster hardware alone cannot enlarge that theorem's scope. No evidence here establishes that this restriction caused declining adoption. [Carpenter and Grossberg, 1987, *A Massively Parallel Architecture for a Self-Organizing Neural Pattern Recognition Machine*, *Computer Vision, Graphics, and Image Processing*, published primary article; §§1–4, 17–20 methods/assumptions inspected](https://sites.bu.edu/steveg/files/2016/06/CarGro1987CVGIP.pdf). Accessed 2026-09-06.

**A documented historical compute obstacle partly addressed at the time.** ART 2-A replaces an iterative component with explicit steady-state operations and treats intermediate learning rates for noise tolerance. The paper identifies expensive simulation of ART 2 dynamics and reports a computationally cheaper approximation under stated regimes. This is evidence that one limitation was already being addressed in 1991, not proof that present hardware resurrected a frozen 1987 method. Its discussion also distinguishes exact fast-learning limits from simulations that have not reached equilibrium. [Carpenter, Grossberg and Rosen, 1991, *ART 2-A: An Adaptive Resonance Algorithm for Rapid Category Learning and Recognition*, *Neural Networks*, published primary article; introduction, §§2.10–3.1 and method description inspected](https://sites.bu.edu/steveg/files/2016/06/CarGroRos1991NNART2A.pdf). Accessed 2026-09-06.

**Abandonment status:** continuing research/software line. A recent reviewed software publication supplies a concrete counterexample to complete abandonment, without validating any general intelligence claim. [Melton, Tanksley and Wunsch, 2025, *Adaptive Resonance Lib: A Python Package for Adaptive Resonance Theory (ART) Models*, JOSS; published software article, publication record inspected](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.07764). Accessed 2026-09-06.

## 4. Qualitative physics: useful partial knowledge is different from missing ontology

**Demand and supplied structure.** Forbus's qualitative process theory reasons about possible physical changes without exact numerical laws. It receives individuals, process vocabulary, quantities, applicability conditions and qualitative influences. The method instantiates processes, decides which are active, resolves influences and analyzes limits where activity changes. Opposing effects sometimes cannot be resolved from available order information. Its completeness-of-process-vocabulary and sole-mechanism assumptions matter: with unknown omitted processes, inference by exclusion can be unwarranted. **2026 inference:** compute cannot determine an unprovided dominance relation from information that does not distinguish alternatives. The useful distinction is insufficient numerical precision versus insufficient causal vocabulary. This paper supplies a representation and reasoning framework; it does not demonstrate autonomous discovery of the whole input model. [Forbus, 1984, *Qualitative Process Theory*, *Artificial Intelligence*, published primary article; §§3.4–3.6 and §6 inspected. The author's hosted PDF filename resembles a thesis label, but the PDF carries the journal article pagination](https://www.qrg.northwestern.edu/papers/Files/QPT-PHD%28searchable%29.pdf). Accessed 2026-09-06.

**A limitation that more compute does not erase.** Yılmaz and Say prove that specified expressive subsets of the QSIM input/output vocabulary preclude an algorithm that both includes every realizable behavior and excludes all spurious ones under their output requirements. Their construction simulates unlimited-register-machine instructions with operating regions; the issue is representational expressiveness and decidability, not slow 1980s hardware. **Scope limit:** this is neither a refutation of all qualitative reasoning nor proof that practical bounded environments are hard. It constrains unrestricted guarantees. It also does not establish that the theorem caused reduced uptake. [Yılmaz and Say, 2006, *Causes of Ineradicable Spurious Predictions in Qualitative Simulation*, JAIR 27, published primary theoretical article, archived on arXiv in 2011; this PDF carries the published journal version, not an unreviewed preprint. §§2–3.1.2 inspected, including construction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.0020). Accessed 2026-09-06.

**Abandonment status:** continuing community. The official 2026 qualitative-reasoning workshop has a programme and accepted papers; its scope includes hybrid quantitative and learned representations. This establishes activity, not a field-wide share or efficacy claim. [QR 2026 organizers, official workshop page; primary organizational source](https://dwolter.github.io/qr26/). Accessed 2026-09-06.

## 5. What this pass has and has not resolved

The old literature separates problems that the programme must also separate: acquiring a representation, maintaining its inferred state, retaining long-term knowledge, and paying the cost of applying it. These notes do not rank those bottlenecks in current AI. The historical causal question remains open: observing technical obstacles and later niche activity does not identify why uptake changed. A later history pass should require contemporary testimony, project records, or measured bibliometrics before attributing decline to scarce compute, scarce data, fashion, or a conclusive technical failure.

Full-text access was obtained for the papers on which the bounded descriptions above rest. Long sources were inspected at their named methods/assumption sections; this is not a claim to have read every page of every cited long paper. No inaccessible abstract is being used as methods evidence. The longer Drescher specification and the expanded Minton study remain specifically useful dependencies below.
