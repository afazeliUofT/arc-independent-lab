## What the supplied paper resolves

The request for the journal paper is fulfilled. It strengthens the evidence that **correct, retained control knowledge can reduce search while raising the cost of each search step**. It also narrows what can be called empirically validated utility: subsequent matching costs and application frequency are measured, but the savings term remains the estimate derived from the rule's original training example. The paper does not provide a full lifetime cost account, and still delegates detailed generators and the training schedule to Minton's earlier book/report. Those two questions remain unresolved at their broader scope; they are not grounds to dismiss the supplied article or request it again.

## Supplied knowledge and scope of learning

Table 1 and its footnote make a strong assumption explicit: the work reported assumes a complete, consistent domain theory, and treats explanations as proofs. EBL is given a target concept, an example, a domain theory and an operationality criterion. It reformulates an already specified concept into descriptions that are intended to be cheaper to recognize. This is not evidence that the system discovered unknown physical laws from observations. [Minton 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(90)90059-9), accessed 2026-09-06, printed 364-366 / PDF 2-4.

PRODIGY is a means-ends planning system whose nodes contain goals and a world state. Rules guide node, goal, operator and binding choices. The implemented explanation targets concern success, failure, a sole viable alternative and goal interference. The targets and example-selection heuristics are supplied. Architectural axioms are hand-crafted; domain-level axioms are derived from the provided operators and existing control rules. Explanation-based specialization maps a selected problem-solving trace into an explanation without searching for alternatives. Optional user-supplied simplification axioms contribute facts about legal initial states that cannot be recovered from the operators alone. Thus the completeness assumption about the explanatory theory should not be misread as a claim that the operator set alone specifies every relevant invariant. [Minton 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(90)90059-9), accessed 2026-09-06, printed 370-374 / PDF 8-12, especially footnote 5 on printed 373.

The limitations are unusually direct. The author describes the architectural theory as highly engineered. The implemented system cannot generate recursive explanations for a relation such as a block being under another block, cannot compare competing explanations, and cannot modify its targets or theory. Some learned rules consequently remain overly specific. These are representational and explanatory limitations even when retained rules remain correct. The discussion provides concrete examples and author analysis, not controlled ablations establishing each limitation's contribution to aggregate performance. [Minton 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(90)90059-9), accessed 2026-09-06, printed 385-386 / PDF 23-24.

## What utility validation actually measures

The utility formula is:

`estimated utility = (average savings estimate × application frequency) − average match cost`.

The initial estimate compares the cost of matching a newly learned rule against the search it would have eliminated on its producing example. Only rules with a favorable initial estimate enter the active set. Subsequent problem solving directly measures application frequency and match cost. **It does not remeasure average search savings.** The author says that counterfactual runs with and without each rule would be too expensive, so the system retains the original example's savings estimate. A rule described in Table 7 as found useful therefore passed this mixed measured/estimated criterion, not an exact counterfactual utility assessment across the future distribution. [Minton 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(90)90059-9), accessed 2026-09-06, printed 375-376 / PDF 13-14; Table 7 on printed 382 / PDF 20.

Utility is also assessed rule by rule. The author explicitly recognizes that adding one rule can change another's application frequency, so the procedure does not optimize the utility of the set. Finally, rules participate in the explanatory theory for subsequent learning: admitting poor rules can change the explanations subsequently generated. These details limit interpretation of validation ablations. Removing validation changes not only the number of retained rules but potentially the future learning trajectory. It is not a clean intervention on matching overhead with a fixed final rule set. [Minton 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(90)90059-9), accessed 2026-09-06, footnote 6 on printed 375 / PDF 13; printed 387 / PDF 25.

## Sampling, generators and evaluation costs

The experiments use blocks world, an extended STRIPS robot domain, and machine-shop scheduling. Procedures randomly generate problems subject to size parameters. Approximately one hundred training problems per domain are presented with progressively increasing maximum size; training subphases permit simpler examples first and subsequent utility evaluation before training ends. Learning is then turned off for one hundred test problems per domain, again with progressively increasing maximum size. A problem can run for at most 80 CPU seconds. The blocks-world range is described as three blocks and three goals through twelve blocks and eight goals. [Minton 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(90)90059-9), accessed 2026-09-06, printed 376 / PDF 14.

These are bounded, progressively sized samples from specified symbolic domains. The journal article does not supply the full generation distributions, training subphase details, seeds, repeated independent train/test draws or uncertainty estimates needed to infer distribution-wide reliability. It expressly delegates generator and training details to reference 23. It does not establish systematic recombination outside the generator, adaptation to changed laws, retention through interference, or unknown-state discovery. Those properties cannot be inferred from the word random or from increasing size. The author also emphasizes that results depend on the particular operator representation and planner architecture, not just on a domain's informal name. [Minton 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(90)90059-9), accessed 2026-09-06, printed 376 and 387-388 / PDF 14 and 25-26.

The plotted costs are **test-phase problem-solving costs**. Training and the one-time learning expense are set aside as less important by assumption. Footnote 7 says learning and problem-solving time were typically within a factor of ten of one another, with substantial variation in both directions. It relates learning expense to explored tree size and the number of selected examples, but supplies no complete per-domain totals or break-even horizon. That observation is not evidence that learning was negligible or that the full learning-plus-use process paid for itself within the reported test horizon. The exclusion is disclosed by the author, not a concealed flaw discovered here. [Minton 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(90)90059-9), accessed 2026-09-06, printed 377 / PDF 15, footnote 7.

The time cap also matters. Unsolved problems contribute capped search rather than a measured completion time. Table 2 separately reports failures to solve within that cap. Consequently the cumulative-time curves are not unconditional time-to-solution curves, and aggregate runtime comparisons cannot be interpreted independently of solution coverage. The author explains the flattening of the no-rules curve in those terms. [Minton 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(90)90059-9), accessed 2026-09-06, printed 377-379 / PDF 15-17.

## Relation to the earlier archaeology note and to 2026

The prior `evidence/SCOUT_ARCHAEOLOGY_2026-09-06.md` accurately identifies the basic utility problem and the fact that test timings exclude learning expense. This source-resolution note adds a material qualification: calling the later step empirical validation without specifying its fixed savings estimate can imply more measurement than occurred. The journal paper confirms that detailed generators remain elsewhere; receiving the expanded article does not close that specific dependency.

The historical objection to a hardware-only answer is explicitly present in section 3.1: increasingly numerous and expressive learned descriptions themselves require matching search. The article invokes worst-case matching complexity and demonstrates practical redundancy arising from macro construction. This is documented contemporary testimony about a technical obstacle. **It is not evidence that this obstacle caused the field to abandon EBL.** No historical uptake analysis is supplied here. [Minton 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(90)90059-9), accessed 2026-09-06, printed 367-370 / PDF 5-8.

**2026 inference, not a contemporary benchmark result:** faster hardware can change the cost constants, feasible rule-set size and frequency of expensive cases. It does not logically make unbounded relational matching free. Conversely, a worst-case complexity result does not show that matching remains the limiting cost on a bounded present-day workload. The measured deficit could also move when architectures, indexing, representations or workloads change. No cross-era speed comparison has been performed. The transferable diagnostic requirement is to distinguish knowledge's availability from its affordable use and to count learning as well as use when making lifetime claims; no particular modern failure has yet been causally assigned to this historical mechanism.

**Consequences for Phase 1, not a ranking:**

- Retained knowledge can fail at application even when no forgetting occurs, but the paper also demonstrates substantial benefits from retained control rules under its supplied-theory conditions.
- A deficit can come from expensive rule application or from insufficient coverage/generality; the scheduling result prevents treating these as interchangeable.
- A system can derive correct rules while lacking the explanatory vocabulary to express a useful generalization. This differs from erasing an already acquired generalization.
- Evidence for successful future use is conditional on the problem distribution, representation, architecture, learning-cost horizon and quality criteria. Increasing problem size alone does not test the programme's full demand.

These are constraints on the diagnosis. They neither establish a dominant cause in current AI nor justify a proposed repair.
