# Raw second population B1 — forming and revising descriptions

2026-09-10. Generated first, after `P2_SECOND_GENERATION_INPUT_034.md`. Novelty and feasibility screening OFF; no clustering or candidate selection. Each entry attacks description formation or revision within diagnosis priority B1. Origin tags refer to the input record and are prompts, not evidence. These are rough hypotheses, including intentionally excessive ones.

**R2-B1-01 — Give an unexplained difference a place before giving it a meaning. [History; inversion]** When two encounters treated as equivalent yield different continuations, create an unresolved distinction attached to those encounters, without immediately naming a hidden variable. Grow candidate observable tests from their earlier histories and bind the distinction only when a test predicts its recurrence. This could let explanatory structure develop from a recorded obligation to distinguish, instead of requiring the correct predicate before the contradiction can be represented.

**R2-B1-02 — Turn an experiment into a word. [History; orthodoxy violation]** Treat a repeatable action-and-observation procedure as a provisional descriptive primitive: an encounter has a property if that procedure produces a stated contrast. Compose such procedures to describe subsequent encounters, preserving that the act of describing can change the world. Vocabulary would then acquire an operational meaning from interaction rather than inherit an evaluator's feature label.

**R2-B1-03 — Promote a history fragment into a state variable. [History]** After a failed prediction, search stored prefixes for a short event pattern that separates otherwise similar encounters. Add the pattern's running status to the learner's state and revisit predictions previously made without it. A hidden switch or delayed effect could become expressible as remembered history before the learner knows what physical entity carries it.

**R2-B1-04 — Discover boundaries where actions stop commuting. [Combination; orthodoxy violation]** Compare the outcomes of action sequences in different orders and build descriptive units around repeatable order effects. Join or separate units according to where those effects appear and disappear under further encounters. This could discover interactions and dependencies without starting with a complete object decomposition.

**R2-B1-05 — Learn what belongs together by trying to replace it. [History; combination]** Exchange parts of previously successful action sequences between encounters and retain the smallest connected pieces whose replacement preserves an observable consequence. Let those pieces become candidate roles or operations in the description language. Failed substitutions would supply new boundaries for recombination instead of merely lowering a whole-episode score.

**R2-B1-06 — Name an absence by how it can be undone. [Biology—evidence access; inversion]** When a previously visible signal disappears, describe its absence through the learner's attempts to restore the opportunity to observe it. Group absences by restoration behavior rather than by identical blank observations. This could create a distinction between different missing-evidence conditions before any of them has a named cause.

**R2-B1-07 — Let successful actions become candidate causes of ignorance. [Biology—evidence access]** Add descriptions of the learner's own accumulated products and interventions when they repeatedly precede a loss of observable variation. Re-express earlier claims conditionally on those self-produced conditions and seek encounters that separate them. The description language could thereby acquire variables about how evidence is obtained, not only about the apparent objects being predicted.

**R2-B1-08 — Learn a relation that survives competing calibrations. [Biology—feedback attribution; combination]** Fit different candidate interpretations of a changed sensor stream and search for action-outcome comparisons on which they still agree. Promote those comparisons into provisional descriptive primitives while retaining their calibration disagreement. A usable relation could emerge before absolute sensory meaning is settled.

**R2-B1-09 — Invent temporal units from recurrent causal delays. [History; orthodoxy violation]** Allow the learner to redraw event boundaries when an effect repeatedly follows an earlier intervention rather than the latest action. Turn the intervening sequence into a provisional process with its own start, persistence and completion conditions. This could make delayed interactions expressible without supplying a fixed episode segmentation that already locates the cause.

**R2-B1-10 — Grow an object from a bundle of controllable consequences. [Combination]** Track sets of observable changes that repeatedly travel together across different permitted manipulations, then introduce a provisional common bearer for the bundle. Split or merge that bearer when later manipulations separate or unite the consequences. Object identity would be a revisable explanatory convenience built from effects rather than a permanently assigned sensory token.

**R2-B1-11 — Let magnitude appear only when order ceases to suffice. [History; inversion]** Begin with coarse descriptions such as different, earlier and reversible. Add counts, distances or continuous quantities only at recurring failures that those coarse distinctions cannot resolve, choosing constructions from the actual interaction trace. This reverses the assumption that a rich numerical state must precede learning the relations that make a quantity useful.

**R2-B1-12 — Invent a conserved quantity out of closed excursions. [History; orthodoxy violation]** Search for combinations of observed changes that cancel when the learner returns through a successful action cycle. Use the recurring cancellation to propose a quantity or balance relation, then carry it into new compositions. The learner could acquire a constraint that organizes unfamiliar transitions without first receiving the world's preferred variables.

**R2-B1-13 — Ask several descriptions to repair the same failure. [Combination]** Maintain separate description builders that extend temporal memory, partition observations, introduce relational roles or change the observation account. Give each the same failed prediction and retain the distinct repairs it constructs. Competition would occur over explanations the learner can actually produce, rather than only over hypotheses supplied complete at initialization.

**R2-B1-14 — Make the grammar itself learn from its failed searches. [Orthodoxy violation]** Record which fragments recur in partially successful explanations even when no complete explanation fits. Promote useful fragments into new construction operators and allow later explanations to use them as primitives. This might reduce repeated rediscovery and let the learner's language change as its encounters expose recurring explanatory work.

**R2-B1-15 — Store a relation between representations before choosing either one. [History; combination]** When two description systems support overlapping predictions, learn a partial translation from the encounters where they meet and preserve the unmapped portions. Let that translation create new shared roles only where further use warrants them. Recombination could begin through a learned correspondence without an early irreversible choice of a single world description.

**R2-B1-16 — Make contradiction enlarge a relation's arguments. [History]** Start with a relation of few arguments; when its use fails systematically, search the local encounter history for another participant, ordering or condition that explains the failure. Extend the relation's argument structure and retain how the new argument was discovered. This could grow relational complexity from concrete failures rather than require all useful arities and roles to be specified beforehand.

**R2-B1-17 — Make descriptions serve questions that disagree. [Inversion; combination]** Let different self-generated questions produce their own local partitions of the same encounters, then learn where one partition refines or conflicts with another. Preserve a way to move between them instead of requiring one representation to be sufficient for every possible demand. An agent might learn a usable family of descriptions from actual differences in informational need.

**R2-B1-18 — Develop a vocabulary through restricted action. [Orthodoxy violation]** Begin with a deliberately restricted subset of the available actions, learn descriptions of what can be changed within it, and reopen additional actions gradually. Revisit the earlier vocabulary whenever an added action separates cases it had merged. Description formation would acquire an ordered history of revisions rather than start with all degrees of freedom competing at once.

**R2-B1-19 — Delete a familiar name to expose the evidence that sustained it. [Inversion]** Temporarily remove a learned symbolic label while preserving the encounters and procedures associated with its use. Ask the learner to reconstruct a usable distinction under another description and retain any structure that the old name had concealed. This could make names replaceable and reveal when apparent knowledge consists only of successful label reuse.

**R2-B1-20 — Keep every sensory fragment as its own theory. [Deliberate extreme]** Create a separate explanatory process for every encountered fragment and let processes communicate only through proposed predictions about one another. Never merge their private descriptions, even when they appear redundant. The excessive decentralization could reveal a route by which a shared relation emerges through communication instead of a central predesigned ontology.

**R2-B1-21 — Rebuild the entire language after every surprise. [Deliberate extreme; inversion]** On each unexpected event, discard the current descriptive primitives and reconstruct a language from the full preserved interaction history. Require old queries to be translated into the rebuilt language as part of reconstruction. This gives revision maximum freedom and makes the price of protecting an initially mistaken vocabulary explicit.

**R2-B1-22 — Permit a new hidden cause for every observation. [Deliberate extreme]** Explain every new encounter with its own latent cause and only later demand that causes be combined to answer a fresh question. Let the pressure of that question determine which invented causes can share an operational role. This deliberately postpones compression so far that relation discovery happens at reuse rather than acquisition.

**R2-B1-23 — Refuse hidden causes altogether. [Deliberate extreme; inversion]** Represent only executable transformations between observed histories and the tests that distinguish their continuations. Build higher-level descriptions by composing those transformations, with no latent object or causal entity permitted. This asks how far useful unfamiliar-world knowledge can develop without solving the naming problem it is often assumed to require.

**R2-B1-24 — Let two incompatible languages teach each other new experiments. [Combination; history]** Have two learners describe the same encounters in deliberately different primitive languages, and exchange only executable questions and observed answers. When one language can express a reproducible distinction the other cannot, let the latter extend itself to account for the exchanged procedure. New descriptive capacity could arise from an interaction between incompatible accounts rather than a teacher supplying the desired representation.
