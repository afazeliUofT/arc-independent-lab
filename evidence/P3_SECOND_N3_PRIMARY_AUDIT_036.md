## 1. Exact N3 operation to which the comparison applies

All statements in this section are deductions from the frozen specification, not reported empirical findings.

| Dimension | Frozen N3, including its required N1 dependency |
|---|---|
| State | N1 transition/history table `T`, selected predicates `Phi`, candidate grammar/inventory, counts, conflicting-pair witnesses and versioned search cursors; recipe library `F`; macro library `M`; recipe utility sums/counts; proposal queue/parents; counter `t`; two N1 snapshots and validation queue. Provenance and archived records consume memory. |
| Inputs | Finite categorical observation tuples and ordered primitive actions; observed tokens supply possible grammar constants; declared episode/block boundaries; stationary deterministic physical RESET. Supplied bounds include `L,W,S,K,V,q` and environment/computation/memory ceilings. No latent state, predicted experiment outcomes, supplied adequate world model, or future task answers. |
| N1 update | Append completed ordinary transitions. Search bounded Boolean history predicates for the greatest strictly positive separation of currently conflicting transition pairs; use size/serialization ties. Append chosen predicates and rebuild all active counts. A key is `(current observation, Phi(history), action)`. Prediction is Laplace-smoothed `(count(y)+1)/(n+D)` over the declared observation space, with incomplete computation reported explicitly. |
| Initial allocation | Execute each single-action word once as a RESET-and-action trial, feed its trace to N1, and seed both libraries; initial utilities are zero. Set `t=0`, then increment before reserving each scored experiment. |
| Subsequent allocation | Every `q`th experiment chooses the first never-completed primitive word of length at most `L`. Otherwise insert a macro, delete a primitive, replace a primitive by a macro, or swap adjacent primitives in an acquired parent. Flatten, length-check and deduplicate. Score a child by its **maximum parent mean utility**, with zero for uncredited parents; apply the frozen length/serialization/parent ties. Choose a never-completed child, falling back to coverage. |
| Credit | Freeze N1 before the full trial and after fitting its completed transitions. Issue `V` additional cyclic primitive validation actions starting at `t mod |A|`, without resetting between them. Both frozen copies predict each actual before-history before the outcome arrives; neither fits validation data. Only after scoring may the live learner append validation transitions. If the trial added a predicate, utility is the exact rational mean of `Brier(before,y)-Brier(after,y)`; otherwise utility is zero. |
| Library update | Add every completed trial word to `F` irrespective of utility. Strictly positive available utility adds **every nonempty contiguous fragment** of that word to `M`. This is not discovery of a minimal relevant fragment. Incomplete validation or a nonnumeric frozen prediction yields no numeric credit and no macros, preserving the actual trace. |
| Reset | A physical trial costs RESET plus each primitive; RESET's observation starts history but is not an N1 supervised action. Volatile memory reset preserves the declared persistent objects. New blocks clear active empirical tables and N1 counts while preserving explicitly historical provenance and potentially reusable syntax. The treatment of N3 utility calibration across blocks needs clarification below. |
| Cost | Warm-up costs `2|A|` environment calls; a scored full experiment costs `1+|w|+V`. Count all N1 search/rebuilding, snapshot storage, prediction, exact rational bit operations, proposal enumeration, discarded computations, trace/provenance and reservation overhead. The frozen direct N1 bound is `O(K P n^2 S)` plus rebuilding; N3 generates up to `O(|F||M|L)` mutation candidates before deduplication. |

This is a finite bounded-word repertoire: at most `sum_{i=1}^L |A|^i` distinct nonempty words. Because selection excludes previously completed trials, each non-warm-up word ordinarily obtains at most one scored trial in a block. A stored utility “mean” does not by itself supply repeated estimation or uncertainty control. Periodic coverage eventually visits the finite word space only if the required resources permit it; the substantive hypothesis concerns the order and value of acquisition under cost limits.

The credit gate is conjunctive, not a predicate-count reward. A new predicate with nonpositive validation improvement adds no macros. Conversely, an improvement without any new predicate gets zero credit. Ordinary count changes and predicate changes both occur between the snapshots, so positive credit is not a causal estimate of feature construction.

## 2. Primary-method maps

### A. Schmidhuber 1991: progress-directed control

Primary: [*Curious Model-Building Control Systems*, IJCNN 1991, pp.1458–1463](https://sferics.idsia.ch/pub/juergen/curioussingapore.pdf), §§2–5, accessed 2026-09-10. Garbled extraction was replaced by six-page OCR; the decisive equations on PDF pp.4–5 were visually inspected.

| Dimension | Primary method | N3 correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| State | Predictor `M`, error/confidence predictor `C`, controller `A`, action/state values `Q,U`. | N1 corresponds to `M`; recipe-credit records provide a different progress estimator and controller state. |
| Inputs | Environment input, chosen action, next input; optionally external reinforcement. | N3 restricts observations/actions and supplies no extrinsic reward. |
| Update | Fit `M,C`; use changing expected prediction reliability; update control values. | N3 computes a paired later-data Brier difference and a predicate gate. |
| Allocation | Learn to favor actions/sequences with expected cumulative improvement; §3.2 uses exploratory action selection. | N3 uses literal mutation, parent-score inheritance and periodic coverage. |
| Reset | No N3-style physical reset/block archive contract specified. | N3 adds that strong affordance and accounting. |
| Cost | Predictor/confidence/controller training and interaction; implementation is open. | This does not supply N3's predicate search or library costs. |

**Primary-text caution:** §3.1 defines error-predictor change as after minus before and maximizes its negative. §3.2 step 2 instead prints external reward plus that change, calling it confidence change. This apparent sign inconsistency remains unresolved; the audit does not silently repair the printed numerical loop. The general progress principle is clear, but exact reproduction requires further implementation evidence.

## 3. Concrete histories, and what they do and do not prove

### 3.1 Reachable N3 trace: useful ordinary learning is deliberately uncredited

Take `A={a}`, declared `O={0,1}`, a stationary deterministic world whose reset and every action return `0`, `L>=2`, `V=1`, `q>=2`, and ample resource bounds. Any valid feature limit is allowed. Start with the specified empty N1 and execute the prescribed warm-up `Trial(a)`. Its sole supervised transition has key `(0,(),a)` and outcome `0`; no conflict exists, so no predicate is selected.

At `t=1` the shortest unexecuted mutation is `aa`. Immediately before its trial, N1 predicts `p_before(0)=2/3`. RESET is not a supervised transition. The two actions in `aa` raise that key's count to three, yielding `p_after(0)=4/5`. The one validation action returns `0`, and the exact paired score improvement is

`2(1-2/3)^2 - 2(1-4/5)^2 = 2/9 - 2/25 = 32/225 > 0`.

Nevertheless, N3's utility is **zero**, and `aa` is not promoted to `M`, because no predicate was added. `aa` enters `F` and can itself be mutated; that is different from being an insertable macro.

The natural ungated paired-progress control credits `32/225` on exactly these snapshots/outcome. A categorical Laplace expert in a single IAC region likewise has declining successive errors; with adjacent unsmoothed error windows its progress becomes positive without any representational split. This is a reward/update distinction on a permitted history, not a superiority claim. It also shows that merely replacing Brier by another loss cannot implement the missing predicate gate.

### 3.2 Why the positive gate still cannot identify the useful predicate

A conditional scoring witness isolates the remaining logical confound. Suppose a trial creates a conflict and a new predicate on one part of the retained transition table, while the predicate has the same value on every record supporting a different validation key. For that validation key, let its pre/post outcome-`0` counts be one and three, with no other observed outcomes. Its score improvement on outcome `0` is again `32/225`; the new predicate contributed no split to that key. N3 nevertheless passes its trial-wide predicate gate and promotes every fragment of the word.

This is a **local state/score counterexample**, not a claim that a candidate run produced that state. It demonstrates why attribution cannot follow from the formula: feature construction elsewhere suffices to unlock count-fitting gains here. Later evaluation would need a matched count-fitting intervention with the relevant feature construction held fixed, plus the frozen diagnostic contrasts. No such intervention has run.

### 3.3 Input obstruction for the library comparator

Consider two deterministic worlds identical through a trial that selects a new predicate, with two-outcome validation predictions `p_before(0)=1/2` and `p_after(0)=3/4`. They differ only on the next previously unobserved validation continuation. For `V=1`, outcome `0` yields utility `3/8`; outcome `1` yields `-5/8`. N3 therefore promotes all fragments in the first history and none in the second. A program-corpus compression update given identical solved programs and task likelihoods has identical inputs in these two cases. Tuning its fixed compression penalty or refactoring bound cannot make its decision depend on the missing validation outcome.

For example, if the positively credited word is `abab`, N3 promotes `a,b,ab,ba,aba,bab,abab`, without individual attribution or compression testing. Turning those trial/validation histories into additional synthesis tasks, or replacing the library objective by N3's reward, would supply a different interface/update. The obstruction concerns the specified comparator; it is not a theorem against arbitrary universal program encodings.

## 4. Tunable special cases and family-versus-whole priority

The comparisons support a narrow distinction between algorithms, not a new principle of curiosity. In the 1991 abstraction, N1 can occupy the predictor slot and acquired words can be viewed as action sequences. N3 is thus within the broad learning-progress-control family even though it replaces the described progress estimator, scheduler and representation state. “It uses sequences,” “it learns experiments,” or “it rewards prediction improvement” are insufficient novelty claims.

The exact paired Brier quantity is an ordinary squared predictive-loss difference when categorical outcomes are represented by one-hot vectors. Its exact rational implementation resolves ties and signs; that precision is useful specification, not by itself a novel learning objective. The substantive additional gate depends on a concrete N1 state event and cannot arise merely by choosing a learning rate, discount or window width in an ungated comparator. However, broad frameworks that permit arbitrary intrinsic rewards can readily *express* such a gate. Expressibility establishes family membership, not that a published method already specified or justified it.

Likewise, a universal programming language could encode all of N3. That is not an equal-input/equal-cost operational reduction until it specifies where histories, N1 search, validation labels, recipe provenance, resets and all computation enter. Conversely, no whole-system novelty follows from failing to find an identical tuple of details in four papers. The audit has no exhaustive priority search over autonomous experimentation, competence-progress goal exploration, developmental skill discovery, evolutionary program search or earlier macro acquisition. This is a concrete coverage limit.

## 5. Historical constraint, 2026 interpretation and remaining cost

The supported historical claim is that progress estimators faced activity-comparison and estimator-stability problems, motivating alternative estimates and representations; IAC provides the dated primary account above. An explanation that the family was discarded because computers were too weak is **not established** by the inspected sources. The current GMC preprint shows an active continuation of the measurement problem, with a different operational signal and explicit limits. Its methods are not merely a bandit: this audit also read its full PPO/dynamics-model integration, while treating neither experiment as a complete antecedent for a growing word library.

For N3, evaluating two fixed predictors on the **same** validation cases avoids comparing an old prediction on one case directly to a new prediction on another. It does not equate the validation distributions reached by different recipes. Local post-trial ease, ordinary fitting, grammar adequacy, inheritance from a lucky parent and promotion of irrelevant fragments remain material risks. The validation horizon also excludes distinctions useful only after longer or different future action sequences.

These are present algorithmic costs, not problems that newer hardware alone removes: finite words grow exponentially with `L`; predicate enumeration and conflicting-pair scoring can dominate; two snapshots and full histories grow retained memory; validation consumes interactions and modifies the state; exact rational arithmetic has bit cost. No CPU/GPU speedup, laptop-sized feasibility or HPC requirement has been measured here. Profiling small/typical/largest cases belongs to later authorized design under the governing budget rule, not this audit.

**Specification boundary needing PI attention:** N3 never explicitly explains how per-recipe utility sums/counts are recalibrated on a declared block change. The shared contract clearly clears N1 active counts and local effect tables, preserves provenance, and allows proposed reusable syntax. It does not explicitly classify old utility estimates or say whether warm-up and coverage history restart. Silently importing old numeric credit as current evidence would be unjustified; silently deleting it would also choose an unstated variant. The single-block comparisons above avoid deciding this. Before a multi-block implementation, the frozen-design owner must resolve this semantics in an explicitly dated successor design.

## Residual claim and weaknesses for integration

**Residual claim:** the frozen N3 defines a particular measurable coupling of N1 historical-predicate acquisition to future experiment construction: gated exact paired Brier improvement on actual additional interactions changes mutation priorities and an executed contiguous-fragment vocabulary. Its difference from the inspected concrete comparator updates is demonstrated; novelty/priority of that coupling is unresolved.

**Strongest weaknesses:** (1) its progress signal belongs to an established family and its library/search feedback has established antecedents; (2) the gate cannot distinguish useful new predicates from incidental count fitting, while all-fragment promotion spreads trial-level credit indiscriminately; (3) no evidence yet shows this local, finite, deterministic-reset procedure obtains useful future distinctions efficiently, and multi-block utility semantics remains incomplete. None is a formal verdict or a candidate-run result.
