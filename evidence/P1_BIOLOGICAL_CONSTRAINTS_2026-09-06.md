# Phase 1: biological persistence requires an inventory of surviving state

**Access date for every source and linked deposit: 2026-09-06.** Bounded primary-method pass by a research sub-agent sharing the PI's tools and filesystem; not independent review. All four main papers are published articles, not preprints. This pass extends `SCOUT_BIOLOGY_2026-09-06.md`; the supplied-method Ryan correction remains authoritative. No engineering mechanism is proposed or ranked.

The useful result is narrower than “biology solves memory.” Four superficially similar persistence claims concern different surviving states and different interventions. None establishes the funded conjunction of discovering an unknown world's structure through selected interactions, retaining useful knowledge across declared discontinuities, and recombining it under controlled novelty.

## BC1. Cortical inheritance preserves an arrangement, with incomplete reliability and human selection

**Source:** Beisson, J. & Sonneborn, T. M. (1965), *Cytoplasmic inheritance of the organization of the cell cortex in Paramecium aurelia*. [Published article/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.53.2.275), [PMC page scans](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC219507/). All eight printed pages, 275–282, visually read, including figures and experimental procedures; upstream culture/staining references were not separately audited.

**Evidence class:** non-neural organism, surgical intervention and lineage microscopy. Grafts came from incompletely separated conjugants, sometimes cut with a micromanipulator. Reversed cortical-unit rows propagated through hundreds of fissions and sexual processes; normal and reversed rows coexisted within cells. Some rows were lost. Apparently indefinite maintenance explicitly required periodic selection. The inherited variable is a spatial arrangement in a continuing cortex, supported by genetic/endoplasmic controls—not a measured inventory of surviving molecules. The authors explicitly lacked evidence about the molecular nature of the structural constraints.

**Constraint and limit:** state includes spatial relationships, not merely component identities. Lineage persistence must disclose experimenter selection. This is experimentally imposed structural inheritance, not self-directed acquisition or novel causal reuse; molecular degradation/replacement was not measured. It cannot support “all molecules turn over while the complete memory survives.”

## BC2. Stable domains coexist with exchange; exchange is not molecular destruction

**Source:** Goehring, N. W., Hoege, C., Grill, S. W. & Hyman, A. A. (2011), *PAR proteins diffuse freely across the anterior–posterior boundary in polarized C. elegans embryos*. [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.201011094), [full primary text](https://rupress.org/jcb/article/193/3/583/36231/PAR-proteins-diffuse-freely-across-the-anterior), [deposited full-text XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3087016/fullTextXML). Results, Discussion, full Methods and main figure legends read.

**Evidence class:** animal-cell imaging, photobleaching, RNAi and acute drug intervention. During the roughly ten-minute maintenance phase, domain positions remain stable while PAR molecules diffuse laterally and exchange with cytoplasm. Estimated membrane residence is approximately 100 seconds. FRAP/iFRAP and varying bleach geometry distinguish exchange from confinement. Boundary measurements used mild myosin-light-chain depletion; drug experiments used permeabilized embryos. Actin disruption spared maintenance-phase segregation, but later anaphase contraction occurred; disrupting microtubules prevented it.

**Constraint and limit:** maintaining a spatial distribution does not require immobilizing its members. This experiment measures movement/exchange of existing proteins, not replacement of every protein through synthesis/degradation. Supplied developmental organization, cell geometry and remaining cytoplasm persist. No acquired world model or learned transfer was tested. The precise molecular account of domain-size control remained unresolved; experimental phase and imposed conditions limit generalization.

## BC3. Bacterial history effects have measurable carriers and expire under dilution

**Source:** Lambert, G. & Kussell, E. (2014), *Memory and Fitness Optimization of Bacteria under Fluctuating Environments*. [Published full article](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1004556), [complete supplementary methods, Text S1](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1004556.s004&type=supplementary). Main experimental Results/Methods/Discussion and all six supplement pages read. The [publication correction](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1004793) concerns author-name typesetting.

**Evidence class:** bacterial microfluidics, fluorescent recording and expression/chemical interventions; additional metabolic modelling. Alternating glucose/lactose exposure reduces later growth lag. LacZ overexpression and, less strongly, LacY overexpression support protein sufficiency. Reporter decay tracks growth dilution; after sufficiently long glucose intervals the lag returns, arguing against fixed constitutive mutants as the explanation. This is residual physiological capacity under an existing regulatory system. It is not an arbitrary new rule learned from action selection. The reported metabolic benefit of the model is simulation output, not measured competitive fitness.

**Method constraints:** the principal growth strain is non-motile, with three deletions reducing aggregation/adhesion. Growth is inferred from displacement averaged across five chambers. Lag boundaries initially selected by hand feed a regression. These details matter: neither autonomous exploration nor an individual-cell memory inventory is established. Reporter maturation also delays readout relative to underlying molecular state.

## BC4. Dormancy leaves chemical carry-over; induction of one response is not complete memory restoration

**Source:** Boussard, A., Delescluse, J., Pérez-Escudero, A. & Dussutour, A. (2019), *Memory inception and preservation in slime moulds: the quest for a common mechanism*. [Published article/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2018.0368), [full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6553583/), [supplement collection](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.c.4431401). Full main article, Methods and figure legends read.

**Evidence class:** non-neural organism, chemical/dormancy intervention, behavioural tracking and sodium assay. Physarum received continuous salt exposure while feeding during six-day training. Reduced salt aversion and elevated sodium survived one-month dormancy, including a control dried without added salt. Two-hour forced salt absorption increased subsequent expansion in salt but did not remove initial pseudopod latency. Thus the authors' “memory inception” interpretation exceeds complete reproduction of both readouts. One-year observations were excluded because survival differed markedly between groups.

**Constraint and limit:** a dormant organism is not an erased organism; a chemical carrier and the organism's evolved response machinery remain. Main methods account for shared plasmodium/sclerotium origins with mixed models. Sodium is implicated but not proved the sole sufficient storage substrate. Broader protein/RNA loss during dormancy is cited background, not measured here. Physiological carry-over supports history dependence, not novel recombination or inferred causal structure.

## Diagnostic consequences, stated as our inferences

These findings constrain how this programme attributes failures; they do not establish that an artificial method violates a biological requirement.

1. **Specify the transformed state before calling an event a discontinuity.** Removing an external stimulus, stopping an organism's activity, moving components, replacing components, dividing a lineage and overwriting an agent's accessible state are different transformations. A comparison needs an explicit surviving channel on both sides.
2. **Specify which equivalence is required.** Equal output at one moment, equal future response over a restricted assay, identical microscopic state, and preservation of arbitrary knowledge are different claims. Persistence of a function can be real without exact microscopic identity; a successful limited assay does not establish preservation of every relevant function.
3. **Account for the origin of useful structure.** Within-lifetime exposure should be separated from inherited response organization, supplied environments, experimental interventions and selection of surviving lineages. Calling the final exposure count a complete acquisition budget would discard those inputs.
4. **Separate natural operation from a diagnostic intervention.** A carrier perturbation or artificially induced response can narrow causal explanations without establishing that the unmodified organism can recover or use the relevant distinction on demand. Conversely, a failed natural readout does not by itself prove absence of every experience-dependent distinction.
5. **Make robustness conditional on the process between observations.** Wall-clock time is not an interchangeable stressor. Drift, dilution, turnover, interference, starvation, state deletion and selection need their own accounting. A frozen digital record and an active dividing lineage do not bear the same retention burden.

These are interpretation requirements, not yet ranked causal bottlenecks in AI. To promote one into a claim about current systems requires an identified artificial task, a declared state inventory and budget, and an intervention that separates that explanation from alternatives. A source from each biological domain cannot substitute for that test.

## Evidence against the broad motivating premise

This pass supplies positive examples of restricted persistence, but no evidence that non-neural organisms effortlessly solve the full funded problem. The earlier crow/toddler action-transfer experiment remains a direct behavioural boundary case in `SCOUT_BIOLOGY_2026-09-06.md`; it should not be generalized into species-wide incompetence either. The present source choices therefore neither validate the initial human/animal superiority claim nor refute narrower forms of it.

The old cortical-inheritance result is particularly useful against romanticizing the archaeology: the paper itself reports losses, reversion and selection. It was not established here that this biological tradition was abandoned, nor why. Its age alone supplies no abandonment story, and present compute does not retrospectively remove its experimental limitations.
