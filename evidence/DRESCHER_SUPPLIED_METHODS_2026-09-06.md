## The disputed specification is now clear

**Correction:** It would be false to describe Drescher's 1989 synthetic-item specification as relying only on elapsed duration, or as lacking prediction-based maintenance. D89 explicitly provides prediction-based updates alongside three other evidence types. This confirms the caution in the earlier archaeology note; it does not justify attributing a duration-only defect to Drescher's whole design. [D89, §3.4.3, pp.125–127](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.

The thesis defines a synthetic item by the otherwise unknown condition that makes an unreliable host schema valid. That definition is distinct from the evolving evidence used to judge whether the condition currently holds. Creation requires the schema to be unreliable but locally consistent: success tends to recur when retried soon. The mechanism estimates that consistency and its typical duration. Persistence-seeking is expressly built into the machinery; the learned part is the particular persistent condition, not the general assumption of persistence. This is a substantive prior, not unrestricted creation of any useful hidden state. [D89, §3.4.3, pp.119–125](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.

| Evidence or condition | Specified effect on a synthetic item | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| Completed host trial | Success sets the item On; failure sets it Off. | D89 p.126 |
| Reliable context spinoff | Applicability supplies evidence that the host would succeed without another host trial; the item is set On. | D89 p.126 |
| Another reliable activated schema predicting the item | In the absence of contrary evidence, a positive result sets it On and a negative result sets it Off. | D89 p.126 |
| Most recent evidence retained over time | On persists for the empirically estimated local-consistency duration unless newer evidence changes it, then becomes Off. | D89 p.126 |
| Prolonged absence of evidence | After several times that duration without evidence, the state becomes Unknown. | D89 p.127 |
| Conflict | Contradictory evidence yields Unknown, except that host-trial evidence overrides conflict; temporal persistence yields to newer evidence. | D89 p.127 |

Table source: [D89, §3.4.3, pp.126–127](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06; both pages visually verified. The wording does not supply a numerical value for “several” on p.127. The table is a prose-method extraction, not a claim to a complete executable specification.

D87 already allowed a synthetic item's positive or negative occurrence in another reliable schema's result to update its state after activation. Thus the thesis provision is consistent with the 1987 account rather than a newly discovered reversal. [D87, printed p.293 / PDF p.4](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1987/AAAI87-052.pdf), accessed 2026-09-06.

## What the later comparison does and does not decide

HI04 Table 1 contrasts predictive updates with average-duration maintenance; §3.2 attributes its original comparator's excess errors to retaining transient states too long. Its extended method also labels a synthetic item's earlier state retroactively from the later host outcome, then learns predictive schemas. The comparison changes discovery, refinement, item-creation and maintenance criteria; its baselines remove synthetic items. [HI04, PDF pp.2–6](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2004/file/18bb68e2b38e4a8ce7cf4f6b2625768c-Paper.pdf), accessed 2026-09-06.

**Inference from the source comparison:** the paper's contrast does not faithfully summarize D89's complete maintenance specification. The published experiments support conclusions about the implemented comparators, but do not establish that Drescher lacked predictive maintenance or isolate maintenance alone from the other changed synthetic-item criteria. The comparator's code and exact update scheduling were not inspected. HI04 cites the 1991 book, which remains uninspected. Retroactive labeling could matter even with predictive updates present in both designs; D89 pp.126–127 do not spell out that same procedure. These are unresolved implementation questions, not evidence that HI04's numerical results are wrong. [HI04, PDF pp.3–6, 8](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2004/file/18bb68e2b38e4a8ce7cf4f6b2625768c-Paper.pdf), accessed 2026-09-06; comparison against [D89 pp.126–127](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.

## What MARCSYST I was actually reported to do

The thesis concerns a genuine implementation, MARCSYST I. It uses a Connection Machine with 4,096 processors available to the programme and a Symbolics front end; most code is *LISP, with inner loops in ParIS. Large per-schema/per-action structures and exhaustive association tables make memory the reported practical limit. The digital architecture is described separately from the hypothetical neural architecture. This establishes the platform and author's identified memory constraint, not source-code verification. [D89, §3.3.2, pp.93–94](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.

The learning interface is heavily structured. The two-dimensional world has uniform-size objects, discrete movement, a supplied bird's-eye visual field, position channels, touch channels and predefined elementary actions. The thesis explicitly says the visual arrangement bypasses hard real-world vision. The central learner lacks supplied semantic meanings for those items, but that does not mean the whole agent begins without engineered perceptual structure. [D89, §3.1, pp.63–69](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.

The reported experimental boundary is narrow:

1. **Pilot rather than quantified repeated-trial evaluation.** Several dozen runs had at least minor changes between them. The chapter gives an informal account of typical results, explicitly declining to quantify between-run consistency and variation. The author calls for independent replication and quantitative characterization. [D89, p.128; §6.2, p.204](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.
2. **The full world exhausted memory early.** It ran out of memory before constructing synthetic items designating persistent objects. A restricted world disabled sideways hand and eye motion and limited the external object to one constrained region. The later persistent-object examples generally come from that restricted mode. [D89, p.129; p.143](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.
3. **Advanced structures were sometimes only nearly acquired.** A visual context for reliably touching an adjacent object was detected, but its schema had not reached the spawning threshold before memory exhaustion. In the object-representation example, one direction of visible/palpable coordination was reported as spawned; the converse correlation was noticed but its schema only almost spawned. A figure showing both directions must not be treated as proof that both were completed. [D89, pp.143–145, 150–151](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06; pp.150–151 visually checked.
4. **There is a concrete context-mediated maintenance example.** The host of a visible-object item develops a reliable context spinoff using the palpable-object item. That permits one inferred representation to supply evidence for the other without another visual probe. It substantiates the reported use of more than a duration timer, but is not a logged, isolated demonstration of every prediction-based branch in the maintenance specification. [D89, §4.3.2, pp.149–151](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.
5. **Some apparent object identity depends on restrictive circumstances.** A peripheral-view synthetic item can preserve a visual detail, but the thesis notes potential confusion between objects when more than one is present. This is a conditional representation achievement, not a demonstrated general solution to identity through occlusion. [D89, pp.148–149](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.
6. **The strongest structures had not been used for substantial tasks.** The evaluation says the mechanism barely reaches those representations and does not yet use them to, for example, grasp an object for a further purpose. The next developmental section is explicitly hypothetical. [D89, §4.4 opening, p.151; §6.3, p.208](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.

## Diagnostic implications, distinguished from historical results

**Inference about what must be separated:** changing or losing the inferred current state of an object is not equivalent to erasing the acquired rule describing how that object behaves. D89 separately stores schema content, reliability data, item state and learned verification conditions. An evaluation of “memory” would need to establish which of these failed. This is a demand for precise diagnosis; the thesis does not establish which failure dominates current AI. [D89, §§3.2.1 and 3.4.3, pp.69–75, 119–127](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.

**Inference about accessible knowledge:** possessing two representations whose referents coincide does not entail being able to use evidence obtained through either one. D89's intermodal example begins with the visible and palpable items potentially disagreeing, then acquires a way to coordinate them. The example distinguishes representation construction from cross-representation use. Its restricted, partly completed implementation does not demonstrate general recombination. [D89, §4.3.2, pp.149–151](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.

**A documented discovery-path difficulty:** D89 describes a schema for which the hand's visual position and the object's visual position are individually unhelpful but their appropriate conjunction predicts touch. Incremental marginal attribution cannot discover the conjunction by that route; a previously formed context from another action model supplies a route to recognizing the remaining condition. The account and its almost-spawned endpoint must remain distinguished. This is evidence that availability of a useful conjunction and reachability by a particular discovery process are different questions. [D89, pp.143–145](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.

**Historical constraint versus 2026 claim:** actual memory exhaustion is well documented. D89 also identifies proliferation of partially useful or redundant schemas and explicitly says the protections against combinatorial growth lack a sufficiency demonstration. The author speculates that a larger machine would enable more development; that speculation is not a cross-era experiment. No result here establishes that extra 2026 compute removes the learning or scaling obstacle, that hardware scarcity caused the programme's later uptake pattern, or that its fundamental construction premise was refuted. Those historical and present-performance questions remain open. [D89, pp.94, 129, 143, 150–151, 204–209](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77702), accessed 2026-09-06.

## Resolution status

- **Resolved:** the 1989 design explicitly includes learned predictive state maintenance, evidence priority and an Unknown state; duration-only is an inaccurate description of that specification.
- **Bounded:** the thesis reports actual restricted-world representation construction and partial coordination, with memory exhaustion and a non-quantitative pilot evaluation.
- **Unresolved:** source-code fidelity, the 1991 book's exact relationship to the thesis, later comparator update semantics, prediction-branch implementation coverage, generalization outside the engineered world, and historical causes of declining uptake.
- **No additional paper is required to support the corrected, bounded conclusion above.** Claims about book-version changes or exact comparative fidelity must remain suspended unless the relevant book passages or original comparator code become available.
