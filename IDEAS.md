## Raw population B1 — selecting a relation that survives reuse

Generated 2026-09-06 after the focused biology pass. Novelty and feasibility screening off. These are hypotheses, including deliberately extreme ones, not findings or selected mechanisms. B1 denotes diagnosis rank 1. Source tags identify prompts for invention, not evidence of efficacy.

**B1-01 — Keep the unresolved disagreement. [History; inversion]** Fit several executable explanations that agree on the available observations but disagree on a later intervention. Keep the disagreement as learned state rather than forcing a single reusable rule; B1 might diminish because present fit would no longer be mistaken for a uniquely warranted relation.

**B1-02 — Learn the variable by what can change alone. [Orthodoxy violation]** Start with raw observation fragments and group them according to which changes can be produced independently through interaction. Build candidate variables from those groups, so the units offered to later learning reflect discovered intervention distinctions rather than appearance alone.

**B1-03 — Make a rule carry its counterexample. [History]** Store each learned rule with the nearest observed case where its extension would be wrong. During application, the rule and counterexample jointly determine scope; this could prevent a locally successful generalization from erasing the distinction needed under reuse.

**B1-04 — Reverse the usual compression target. [Inversion]** Compress observations aggressively but preserve disagreement between the decisions licensed by alternative explanations. Two histories would be merged only when a declared family of future probes cannot tell their implications apart, potentially selecting reusable decision distinctions instead of reconstructive similarity.

**B1-05 — Scramble names while keeping events. [Orthodoxy violation]** Repeatedly change the arbitrary identifiers of entities in stored experiences while preserving their observed participation in events. Require a learner to predict through these relabelings; relations would have to survive changes to names without assuming that every spatial or sensory change is irrelevant.

**B1-06 — Treat the observer as another unknown. [Biology]** Learn competing explanations in which a mismatch changes the world model, the observation relation, or both. Commit an update to a reusable relation only to the degree that the actual evidence separates those explanations, addressing the risk of learning a sensor disturbance as a world law.

**B1-07 — Leave solved cases exposed as controls. [Biology; inversion]** When current competence reduces exposure to a formerly common event, retain occasional access to its observation conditions. Use those controls to distinguish successful prediction from self-produced censorship of evidence before deciding that a broad learned relation has become reliable.

**B1-08 — Separate usefulness from reliability. [History]** Give a candidate rule different records for how often it helps obtain reward and how accurately its stated conditional prediction holds. Select reusable relations from the latter record even when a frequent profitable shortcut dominates the former.

**B1-09 — Keep every exact encounter separate. [Deliberate extreme]** Refuse all generalization during acquisition and keep each interaction as a unique executable case. At reuse, construct a relation only for the immediate query; this attacks premature selection by postponing it as far as possible, even if its costs are enormous.

**B1-10 — Generalize everything immediately. [Deliberate extreme; inversion]** After each interaction, assert the broadest relation the current description language can express, then let the next contradiction split it. The deliberately excessive commitment could turn the location of failure into a source of candidate variables and scope boundaries.

**B1-11 — Negotiate a shared role through different descriptions. [Combination]** Give several learners the same interaction through different observation encodings and require them to agree on predictions about future action sequences. A reusable role would be the correspondence needed for agreement, rather than a coordinate selected by one encoder's training loss.

**B1-12 — Compile only explanations that survive substitution. [History; combination]** Before converting an observed solution into a reusable instruction, replace one participant with another for which relevant evidence exists. Keep which substitutions succeeded and failed as part of the instruction, so compilation preserves a discovered domain of validity.

**B1-13 — Train on disagreement between future questions. [Orthodoxy violation]** Generate a declared range of possible later questions from the current world description and identify where equally well-fitting representations answer differently. Allocate learning effort to those disagreements, without disclosing the eventual evaluation questions or insisting that one description reconstruct everything.

**B1-14 — Let a contradiction create a new predicate. [History]** When all existing conditions assign two experiences the same state but their effects differ, introduce a candidate predicate from their observable difference. Recheck old rules under the enlarged description rather than absorbing the contradiction into an average prediction.

**B1-15 — Learn relations between changes. [Orthodoxy violation]** Predict how the effect of one intervention changes after another intervention, before trying to name either hidden cause. A representation of these second-order effects might support recombination even when the learner never obtains the experimenter's preferred object ontology.

**B1-16 — Preserve negative evidence with its sampling route. [Biology; combination]** Record that an expected event was absent together with the action and sensing conditions that made its absence observable. A later learner could then distinguish an unsupported rule from an untested one when its own behavior changes those conditions.

**B1-17 — Give every context a private theory. [Deliberate extreme]** Permit no parameter or rule sharing between contexts and learn explicit translations only when a reuse attempt demands them. This sacrifices immediate transfer to prevent a high-frequency context from selecting the interpretation of a lower-frequency one.

**B1-18 — Force one tiny theory to cover everything. [Deliberate extreme; inversion]** Impose an extremely small executable description while retaining all unresolved residuals outside it. If the common theory cannot silently average away those residuals, its pressure toward simplicity might expose which new distinction is needed rather than reward a large collection of shortcuts.

**B1-19 — Pay rent in failed predictions. [History; combination]** Let stored candidate relations compete for representation space according to the distinct predictive errors they eliminate, rather than their raw firing frequency. A rarely applicable relation can survive when it accounts for a kind of event no common relation explains.

**B1-20 — Separate evidence for existence from evidence for identity. [Orthodoxy violation]** Allow a learner to infer that two independent causes are needed without yet assigning either a stable name or sensory location. Delay identity binding until further observations warrant it, preventing a useful count or structure from becoming attached prematurely to the wrong observable feature.

**B1-21 — Follow a rule through its own interventions. [Biology]** Evaluate a relation under the altered observation process produced when the learner acts on that relation. If acting on it removes its confirming evidence, maintain that dependency rather than taking the remaining easier observations as confirmation that the relation is universally valid.

**B1-22 — Learn only comparative consequences. [Inversion]** Replace absolute predictions with claims that one allowed action produces more, less or the same consequence as another under stated conditions. Such partial relations may remain reusable when absolute calibration changes, while leaving unanswered questions visible.

**B1-23 — Enumerate descriptions without gradients. [Deliberate extreme; history]** Enumerate executable programs over the actually available action and observation interface and retain all that fit the interaction record. Use differences among their predicted compositions as the learning state, even if the set is immense and the eventual implementation is impractical.

**B1-24 — Split a learned relation when its evidence type changes. [Combination]** Keep observational co-occurrence, agent intervention and externally imposed feedback as distinct sources of support. When a reusable rule crosses between them, require an explicit bridge hypothesis rather than allowing a training objective to treat them as interchangeable examples.

## Raw population B2 — obtaining the evidence that later decisions need

Generated 2026-09-06 after completion of raw B1. Novelty and feasibility screening remain off; no clustering has begun. B2 denotes diagnosis rank 2. These entries are possibilities, not claims that the proposed actions are affordable or informative in every world.

**B2-01 — Choose the experiment that separates two live explanations. [History]** Retain a pair of explanations that fit current observations but prescribe different future actions, and choose an available intervention on which their observations differ. This directs interaction toward the missing distinction rather than toward the uncertainty of a single fitted predictor.

**B2-02 — Explore what competence has hidden. [Biology]** Keep a record of observation opportunities suppressed by the current successful policy, then sometimes restore those opportunities. This could expose a distinction that disappeared from the learner's data because its own actions masked it, rather than because the distinction ceased to matter.

**B2-03 — Move action and observation in different ways. [Biology; combination]** Choose a sequence that varies the action effect while leaving a suspected observation distortion fixed, then a complementary sequence if available. Such contrasts might distinguish a world change from a channel change before the agent spends its learning budget correcting the wrong component.

**B2-04 — Keep a reversible route to an informative state. [Orthodoxy violation]** Value actions partly for preserving access to a state from which unresolved alternatives could later be tested. Exploration would acquire an option to obtain evidence, rather than always purchasing the evidence before its future usefulness is known.

**B2-05 — Spend the whole budget on one disputed distinction. [Deliberate extreme]** Select the live ambiguity with the greatest conceivable future consequence and devote every remaining interaction to it. This deliberately sacrifices broad competence to expose whether the evidence gap can be closed at all when immediate reward no longer competes with it.

**B2-06 — Refuse to pursue current reward. [Deliberate extreme; inversion]** During acquisition, choose actions only for how different their predicted consequences are across explanations. A later goal could benefit from distinctions that a reward-seeking policy would never have encountered, even though this behavior may be disastrous under other demands.

**B2-07 — Randomness that remembers where it has been. [History]** Sample action sequences from a distribution that downweights previously repeated observation conditions, regardless of present prediction error. This would buy coverage of different opportunities for evidence instead of repeatedly revisiting a familiar noisy event.

**B2-08 — Enumerate every finite experiment. [Deliberate extreme]** Execute all action sequences up to a growing length bound and retain their observable consequences. This replaces an assumed exploration heuristic with exhaustive exposure, preserving even the histories whose importance cannot yet be explained.

**B2-09 — Borrow experiments from competing tasks. [Combination]** Maintain several internally proposed goals and let each pursue actions useful for itself while sharing the resulting observations. A task that is irrelevant to the current reward might produce exactly the intervention another task needs for later reuse.

**B2-10 — Explore for future questions, not future rewards. [Inversion]** Sample questions from a declared broad family about the world and choose actions that would change their answers. The questions need not be the eventual evaluation goals; their role is to make some future informational demands explicit without supplying the target task.

**B2-11 — Keep searching after success. [Biology; inversion]** Continue selected experiments after the present task succeeds, especially where that success depends on unresolved assumptions. This could prevent reward attainment from terminating acquisition before a still-relevant causal distinction has ever been tested.

**B2-12 — Investigate an absent effect. [Biology]** When an expected event disappears, choose an intervention that could restore its opportunity to occur. A recovered event would discriminate suppression by the agent from a change in the underlying world, instead of letting absence automatically end exploration.

**B2-13 — Pair an experiment with its control in time. [History]** Surround an informative action with repeated reference actions under otherwise similar accessible conditions. Comparing these outcomes could separate action-specific information from drift that occurred while the learner was experimenting.

**B2-14 — Seek disagreements that do not yet affect reward. [Combination]** Identify explanations with the same current optimal action but different outcomes under a held-out composition of actions. Probe those differences during acquisition so the learner does not wait until the composition becomes obligatory to discover that it is unidentified.

**B2-15 — Change the sensor before changing the world. [Orthodoxy violation]** When the interface offers observation choices, spend actions altering viewpoint, measurement timing or resolution while holding the task action fixed. The resulting contrasts might reveal that apparently identical world states were observationally aliased.

**B2-16 — Treat time without action as an experiment. [Inversion]** Deliberately pause physical intervention and observe whether the relevant effect persists, decays or returns. This uses permitted waiting to distinguish autonomous dynamics from effects sustained by the learner's own activity.

**B2-17 — Make the exploration policy contestable. [History; combination]** Maintain competing policies that predict which experiment will resolve a named ambiguity, then compare their claims with the observations actually obtained. Learn when an apparent information-seeking action merely revisits the same unresolved alternatives.

**B2-18 — Restore a previous observation opportunity. [Orthodoxy violation]** Learn action sequences that reconstruct the conditions of an earlier unresolved encounter. This attacks evidence that was obtainable once but became inaccessible after the learner changed the world, rather than assuming that replaying a stored observation recreates an experiment.

**B2-19 — Give rare causal paths their own interaction allowance. [Deliberate extreme]** Assign equal opportunity to each discovered way an action can affect an observation, even when some paths are seldom useful. The learner could acquire minority distinctions that a frequency-weighted acquisition policy systematically omits.

**B2-20 — Return an unresolved question with the action. [Combination]** Let every task action carry a specific question its outcome might answer, and update whether the outcome actually distinguished the alternatives. Over time, select action-question pairs whose realized evidence differs from merely imagined informativeness.

**B2-21 — Explore through a second learner's habits. [Orthodoxy violation]** Let another learner with a different policy act through the same permitted interface and compare which observations each policy systematically misses. Their disagreements could reveal self-created gaps without presuming that either possesses the correct model.

**B2-22 — Buy a calibration before accepting a new discovery. [Biology]** On a surprising observation, choose an action with an established but revisable response relation before treating the surprise as a new world law. Agreement or disagreement across the two encounters can constrain whether the response channel shifted, provided the calibration itself is not treated as an infallible oracle.

**B2-23 — Search for experiments that fail differently. [History]** When every available hypothesis predicts task failure, choose an action for which their failure observations differ. Poor immediate outcomes could still acquire the distinction that makes later success possible, rather than being indistinguishable negative rewards.

**B2-24 — Let inquiry challenge its own stopping rule. [Inversion; combination]** Before ending acquisition, construct an available continuation under which some live explanation would change the later decision. Preserve the unresolved case if such a continuation exists, so stopping reflects an explicit choice about evidence cost rather than the mere availability of an answer.

## Raw population B3 — making a surviving distinction usable after change

Generated 2026-09-06 after completion of raw B2. Novelty and feasibility screening remain off. B3 denotes diagnosis rank 3. The requested observer follow-up is a concurrent diagnostic source of questions, not an efficacy result or a candidate selected in advance. Every proposed surviving channel below would have to be declared and counted.

**B3-01 — Carry a translation between representation versions. [History; combination]** When learning changes an internal description, learn how old queries map into the new one and retain the translation. This could preserve use of an already acquired distinction without requiring every old coordinate or response path to stay fixed.

**B3-02 — Learn which changes leave an old distinction invariant. [Diagnostic prompt]** Observe the learner's own updates and estimate directions along which a particular distinction does not change. At reuse, read the current state through that estimated invariant rather than through a geometry supplied by an outside analyst.

**B3-03 — Give stored knowledge a test of its interface. [Orthodoxy violation]** Preserve unlabeled probe inputs and structural relations between their expected responses alongside a learned item. Reuse would first check whether the current response interface still respects those relations, exposing a broken route before treating its output as the remembered content.

**B3-04 — Never update a state that has been used. [Deliberate extreme]** Freeze every learned state after its first successful use and allocate all subsequent learning elsewhere. Old expression would stay available literally, at the cost of unbounded growth and a new problem of selecting the relevant state.

**B3-05 — Keep only reversible updates. [Orthodoxy violation]** Represent each learning change as an invertible transformation and preserve the information needed to undo selected changes. An old query could then be evaluated in a reconstructed earlier state without claiming that the inverse is free or that its stored information is not memory.

**B3-06 — Store changes, not snapshots. [Inversion]** Retain a description of the effect of each update on reusable responses rather than copying the whole old model. Later access could subtract only the irrelevant response changes while keeping new knowledge that does not conflict with the query.

**B3-07 — Let a reader learn faster than its representation. [Combination]** Train a separate query-to-response route to track changes in a slower learned state, using explicitly retained self-generated probes. The reader would learn the moving interface rather than force the knowledge-bearing state itself to remain immobile.

**B3-08 — Let the representation track a fixed reader. [Inversion]** Hold a reusable query interface fixed and change the learned representation so its relevant distinctions continue to reach that interface. This reverses the preceding division of labor and makes the preserved object the meaning of the query rather than its internal implementation.

**B3-09 — Keep several plausible readings alive. [History]** After interference, evaluate a surviving state through multiple candidate readouts and retain their disagreement. New unlabeled structural constraints or permitted interaction could select among them instead of assuming the original readout still uniquely expresses the old relation.

**B3-10 — Reconstruct access from a new encounter. [Biology; combination]** When a natural query fails, seek a permitted new experience predicted to reactivate or identify the relevant relation. This deliberately uses extra interaction to make latent history useful, and must be distinguished from recovery without new evidence.

**B3-11 — Keep old knowledge in the world. [Deliberate extreme]** Use actions to leave an external arrangement that later cues the needed distinction. This replaces internal persistence with a declared environmental channel and asks whether the actual discontinuity preserves that arrangement.

**B3-12 — Erase the reader after every boundary. [Deliberate extreme; inversion]** Discard the ordinary readout while keeping the learned state, then relearn access from allowed current observations. If useful access can be reconstructed cheaply, retaining a stale reader may have been a source of failure rather than a necessary protection.

**B3-13 — Preserve outputs as well as weights. [History]** Keep a selected set of old inputs and the learner's own responses, then use them to detect or repair later interface drift. This may anchor old behavior directly; the stored responses are an additional history-bearing channel and cannot be called evidence-free recovery.

**B3-14 — Keep a distinction in several incompatible forms. [Combination]** Encode the same acquired relation through different update geometries and observation descriptions. Later learning may disrupt some routes while leaving another usable, with a selection problem that must be solved rather than assigned to an evaluator.

**B3-15 — Match the relation before retrieving its name. [History]** Search current state for a structural pattern implied by the query before choosing a stored identifier. This could recover knowledge whose original label or index no longer points to its surviving expression.

**B3-16 — Store the reason a relation is irrelevant. [Orthodoxy violation]** Alongside a retained relation, record which observable conditions make it inapplicable without making it false. Later action could suppress inappropriate expression while retaining the relation for a different arrangement, avoiding deletion as the only way to stop misuse.

**B3-17 — Split before incompatible updates accumulate. [History]** When two sets of observed consequences require incompatible changes to the same response route, retain separate evolving states and a learned condition for choosing between them. The split might keep both acquired distinctions usable while leaving their common structure open to later recombination.

**B3-18 — Refresh access through use, not repetition. [Biology; inversion]** Use a stored relation in a different currently available situation and retain the newly established access path. This could preserve a function through changing contexts without requiring an exact replay of the original encounter.

**B3-19 — Keep a map of failed queries. [Combination]** Record which queries stopped expressing a previously usable relation and what recent changes preceded that failure. Recovery can then target a bounded conversion problem instead of searching the entire learned state or assuming everything was erased.

**B3-20 — Publish a stable internal language. [Orthodoxy violation]** Require each learned component to expose consequences through a small explicitly versioned language while permitting its hidden implementation to change. Recombination would operate through the stable interface, and failures would identify where a translation no longer preserves the promised relation.

**B3-21 — Allow temporary destructive reading. [Deliberate extreme]** Copy the current state, modify the copy aggressively to expose a suspected old distinction, read the result and discard the copy. This spends extra computation and temporary storage on access while preserving the acting state; the transformations must not import the old answer.

**B3-22 — Stop trying to preserve the answer. [Inversion]** Retain a short procedure for obtaining the relevant distinction again from the current world instead of preserving its value. This may support the same later decision across a boundary, while openly shifting the burden from latent recovery to renewed acquisition.

**B3-23 — Make competing readouts explain their disagreement. [Combination]** When two routes give different old responses, seek a decomposition of current state into changes that affect only one route and changes that affect both. The decomposition may reveal whether a shared stored distinction survived while the paths interpreting it diverged.

**B3-24 — Let every query rewrite its own access path. [Deliberate extreme]** On each use, build a fresh program that maps the surviving state and current observations into the requested response, then retain that program only if subsequent permitted checks support it. This eliminates a permanently privileged decoder and makes its continuing correctness a learning problem.

The largest conceptual overlap is between preserving an unresolved distinction and choosing an action that could resolve it. Those can share a hypothesis substrate but change different parts of the causal chain. Many suggestions about exploring after success, restoring an absent effect and reconstructing an observation opportunity are variants of the same concern: competent action can remove later evidence. Several access proposals differ mainly in which side of a changing interface is kept fixed. Their names and biological prompts do not make them independent mechanisms.

The diversity decision is therefore conservative: four operations across three broad causal families. C1 and C4 both concern relation selection. C1 preserves all compatible explanatory states and exports disagreement; C4 selects the applicability of a proposed local prediction while preserving contradictory witnesses. C2 changes the acquired evidence with its predictive substrate held fixed. C3 changes access while keeping encoder updates and the old reader fixed. The resulting interventions are distinct, but no claim of four orthogonal theories follows. Specification source: `docs/P2_OPERATION_SPECIFICATIONS.md`, SHA-256 `d37a52eeb569461503b262d2790a656048624ede4790aeb49254e5a9f133c26f`.

| C2: decision-dependent experiments | Gives the acquisition claim an explicit action-to-evidence-to-decision chain | Correct models, the actual future test schedule or free interventions |

I chose these for different causal interventions and for specifications whose assumptions can be inspected. I have not ranked them by an expectation of novelty or by laptop affordability. There is an important limitation: no selected operation, on its own, solves construction of an unrestricted useful description language from raw interaction. That unresolved part of B1 remains represented by B1-02/14/15/20 and their cluster. A finite supplied executable family avoids supplying semantic factor names, but still supplies a language. This is a scope limit to carry into the next decision, not a claim that the omitted problem is solved.

The other families remain unrefuted: exploratory coverage, explicit observation-channel identification, altered training criteria, learned descriptions, inquiry self-checks, separated states, reversible updates, external arrangements and reacquisition. None was killed as already known, impractical or biologically wrong. They remain available if the later audit or design reveals that the developed set misses the diagnosis.

### Shared conventions

Every operation has an explicit interface. Supplied alphabets, hypothesis families, goals, representations and context boundaries count as prior structure. Calling a state variable learned does not explain how its input was obtained. A later experiment must expose or control each such dependency. The specifications below intentionally describe narrow mathematical objects precisely rather than claim an implemented general agent.

All tie breaks use a fixed lexicographic ordering of identifiers supplied before a run. Undefined or unsupported queries return a typed `unresolved` value; they do not acquire an arbitrary confident answer. Numerical tolerances, regularization constants, capacity bounds and evaluation thresholds remain design parameters to be chosen and frozen in Phase 3 before measurement. No candidate experiment is run in this phase. Cost symbols describe operations; they are not measured runtimes or affordability judgments.

### C1 — Preserve the alternatives that present evidence cannot distinguish

**Diagnostic target and generated origin.** B1: a training criterion selects one presently successful relation when different future consequences remain compatible with the evidence. Primary origin B1-01, with B1-23's executable descriptions and B1-06/B1-24's distinction between a world relation and an observation relation. The underlying move is retaining unresolved explanatory alternatives, not merely lowering confidence on one predictor.

**Interface and supplied structure.** Use a finite action alphabet A and finite observation alphabet O; their construction is outside this operation. Supply a finite, canonically ordered set H0 of deterministic executable transducers. Each h has a finite state set Qh, an initial-state subset Ih, a transition function Th(q,a), and an emitted observation Oh(q,a). No semantic factor names are required. H0 may contain explanations that assign a changed observation to different world or sensor processes, but inclusion of those alternatives is a declared modeling choice. This is not unrestricted causal-variable discovery.

**State.** A set S of live pairs (h,q), initially all h in H0 with q in Ih. Store each fixed program h once plus its surviving state identifiers. Also retain the fixed prior ordering and an `inconsistent` flag. No raw interaction archive or old target labels are needed for the update itself. Each pair represents an explanation and its state after the entire observed history.

**Inputs and update.** On the actual observed action a and subsequent observation o:

1. For each live (h,q), compute predicted Oh(q,a).
2. If it equals o, include (h,Th(q,a)) in Snew; otherwise remove that pair.
3. Deduplicate identical pairs. Replace S with Snew.
4. If Snew is empty, set `inconsistent` and return `unresolved`. Do not silently relax constraints, invent a new program, restart from H0 or select the least bad survivor.

The strict deterministic rule is the specified candidate. A noisy extension would be a different specified variant, not an implementation detail silently added after results. A fixed observation channel can be part of h; a noise-free comparison cannot establish robustness to arbitrary corrupt labels.

**Query and reuse.** Given a finite future action word u, simulate it from each live pair and return the set of predicted observation words. Agreement licenses that prediction under H0; disagreement remains explicit. If a task asks for a decision d from a finite set D, a supplied decision functional maps each predicted observation word to a loss. Return a decision only under an externally declared commitment rule; the operation's output remains the full alternative set. A downstream policy cannot be credited with this operation if it discards the alternatives and acts on the first surviving program.

**What changes and what stays unchanged.** This changes the content of the learned state and the query interface. It does not choose actions, enlarge H0, change the observation interface, supply a future goal schedule or protect model weights from arbitrary updates. A comparison that also changes the acquisition policy would test a package rather than this operation alone.

**Discontinuity.** Retain S and the program definitions across an episode/context clearing. For an explicit world reset, supply a fixed relation R_h(q) of allowed post-reset states and replace each live (h,q) with (h,qprime) for qprime in R_h(q), then deduplicate. Retain h; never repopulate an already empty inconsistent set from H0. A reset to compatible initial states is one declared R_h, not a default that ignores the observed history. Do not assume that an agent-memory reset and a world reset mean the same operation. Unknown reset behavior must itself be represented in H0 or cause unresolved predictions.

**Cost.** Let K be the number of live pairs and c be the maximum one-step program cost. Updating costs O(Kc), apart from deduplication. A length-L query costs O(KLc), with output storage proportional to the returned alternatives. Model definitions and current state pairs are persistent memory; constructing H0 may be vastly more costly and is not hidden in the update bound.

**How we would know it addressed B1.** Under identical evidence, supplied families and acquisition policy, a comparison learner commits to a relation that fits the record but fails under a warranted reuse. This operation must retain the relevant alternative and make its disagreement available to the actual decision procedure before failure. Improved calibration alone is insufficient. If the useful explanation is outside H0, or every explanation makes the same wrong reuse prediction, this operation has not addressed the case. Correctness under all surviving programs remains relative to the supplied family, not proof of the true ontology.

### C2 — Choose experiments for the decisions their observations could change

**Diagnostic target and generated origin.** B2: current performance selects a history that omits an obtainable, later-relevant distinction. Origins B2-01, B2-02, B2-10, B2-12 and B2-14. The underlying move is changing action selection according to the realized value of possible evidence, including absence produced by earlier actions. Its representation and inference substrate are inputs, not an additional claimed invention.

**Interface and supplied structure.** Supply a finite belief over world states h with probabilities p(h), finite experiments E, and a finite family G of possible future decision problems with weights mu(g). Each experiment e is a bounded policy tree over permitted actions and observations, not an oracle intervention on a hidden variable. For every e, the substrate supplies a finite joint kernel Q_e(hprime,z|h) for the post-experiment world state hprime and observable transcript z, conditional on the current world state h. It also predicts costs and losses of future decisions. These models must include any observation opportunities changed by the learner's actions. An impossible-to-observe absence cannot be supplied as a hidden informative token.

**State.** Current p, current physical/latent state per hypothesis, the remaining interaction allowance, and the immutable definitions of E and G. Store a record of the chosen experiment, predicted transcript alternatives and the actual transcript for auditing. This operation need not generate or repair its predictive models. C1 could provide a substrate after an explicitly supplied prior, but C2 must also be specifiable with a fixed external substrate so their contributions can be separated.

**Exact selection rule.** For each e and possible transcript z, compute P(z|e) = sum over h,hprime of p(h) Q_e(hprime,z|h). For P(z|e)>0, form p_e(hprime|z) = [sum over h of p(h) Q_e(hprime,z|h)] / P(z|e). Zero-probability transcripts contribute zero to the prospective sum. If an actual transcript has zero predicted probability, return model-inconsistent rather than fabricating a posterior. Let

R(e,z) = sum over g of mu(g) times min over permitted d of sum over hprime of p_e(hprime|z) times L_g(hprime,d).

Let R0 be the corresponding expected Bayes decision loss if the agent ends experimentation now. The criterion is

V(e) = R0 - sum over z of P(z|e) R(e,z) - lambda times expected interaction/resource cost of e.

Include `stop`, with value zero and no experiment, in E. Choose the feasible member with maximum V, breaking ties by fixed ordering. Feasible here means it obeys the explicit interface and remaining allowance, not that the PI has screened the research candidate for practicality. Every executable branch of a feasible experiment must fit within the hard remaining interaction allowance; an expected cost alone does not satisfy that bound. Execute only the selected experiment, charge its realized interaction cost, and condition on the actual transcript. Stop that experiment and return model-inconsistent on an out-of-model transcript, preserving the actions and cost already incurred. Replan thereafter only if the model remains consistent. The loss is evaluated in the resulting world state, so an experiment that changes the world is not incorrectly treated as a free observation of its previous state.

**No privileged future task.** G and mu are an explicit prior over future demands, not the realized future evaluation schedule. If the actual task is disclosed at decision time, use the corresponding g then. Acquiring arbitrary information against completely unrestricted unknown future goals is not promised. A candidate implementation cannot inspect withheld tasks to construct G.

**Feedback and biological constraint.** A restoration or withdrawal action is informative only if the actual predictive family distinguishes its transcripts under alternative causes of absence. The rule does not automatically reward every surprising event, nor presume that masking occurred. Earlier action products enter the predicted observation process as state if they matter; ignoring them can make the estimated value of an experiment wrong.

**What changes and what stays unchanged.** This changes which permitted experiment is selected and therefore which data actually arrive. It leaves the predictive family, parameter update, memory allowance and eventual decision loss unchanged. Its gain must not be attributed to acquisition if it receives better models, extra outcomes, more interaction or a revealed future goal.

**Discontinuity and cost.** Persist p and relevant experiment outcomes through the declared memory channel; apply the declared world reset to each hypothesis state. With K current states, Kprime reachable post-experiment states, J experiment policies, Z possible transcripts per policy, G future problems and D decisions, direct enumeration costs O(JZ(KKprime + GKprime D)) when each kernel entry is available at constant cost, plus kernel construction/simulation cost; all symbols are bounds on supplied finite objects. Tree construction, model inference and growing histories are separately charged. No laptop-sized implementation is assumed.

**How we would know it addressed B2.** The chosen experiment must generate an observation that distinguishes the relevant alternatives and changes a later decision, while an equal-allowance comparator's actual record does not. Replaying the acquired record into the same fixed downstream learner should account for the gain. If success comes from a different downstream inference rule, extra goal disclosure or changing the physical world into an easier task without acquiring the alleged distinction, that is a different causal explanation. A computed high V that produces no useful evidence is not evidence of repair.

### C3 — Learn a translation from the current representation to an old query interface

**Diagnostic target and generated origin.** B3: useful historical structure survives but its old response path is no longer aligned with the learned representation. Origins B3-01, B3-03 and B3-07. The move is adapting access to a changed representation, while keeping the underlying new-learning updates fixed. It is separate from the requested observer diagnostic and is not selected on that diagnostic's result.

**Interface and supplied structure.** Supply an encoder f_theta(x) in R^d and a previously learned matrix readout W in R^(d by p) for a declared old query family, with any final decision postprocessing fixed. An explicit boundary identifies when this interface is established. The candidate does not discover that boundary, decide which old relation remains true, or construct the old semantic readout. Select n unlabeled anchor inputs by a deterministic rule over observations available before the boundary, fixed in advance. No held-out probe or evaluator answer may become an anchor.

**State at the boundary.** Store the anchors X=(x_i), their reference activations r_i=f_theta_boundary(x_i), the old readout W, and a translation T initially equal to the identity. R is the matrix with rows r_i. These activations can encode old answers: they are an explicitly counted history-bearing channel, even though no external target labels are stored. The operation must never be described as recovery from current weights alone.

**Update rule.** Continue the original encoder training unchanged. At a fixed refresh schedule, compute current anchor activations Z with rows f_theta(x_i), then set

T = argmin_T ||ZT-R||_F^2 + lambda ||T-I||_F^2,

with a fixed positive lambda. Equivalently solve (Z^T Z + lambda I) T = Z^T R + lambda I. The positive regularizer defines a unique solution; do not silently switch to a different alignment or tune lambda using evaluation answers. On an old query x, the response vector is f_theta(x) T W, followed by the fixed decision postprocessing. The new task continues to use its original response path.

**What changes and what stays unchanged.** Only the old query's conversion from current features changes at refresh. The encoder update rule, new-task reader, old W, anchors and R stay fixed within this candidate instance. Anchors that the changing world no longer makes meaningful are a stated failure condition, not grounds to replace them from a held-out set. Updating W as well would be another operation.

**Discontinuity.** Clearing transient context retains X, R, W and T if those are the declared channel. If they are erased, the candidate has not retained its required state. A world reset does not automatically invalidate them, but changed observation semantics can; a successful translation of fixed inputs is insufficient to establish validity of their old world interpretation.

**Cost.** A refresh uses n encoder evaluations, O(nd^2+d^3) dense linear algebra and O(nd+d^2) numeric memory, in addition to raw anchors and W. A query adds O(d^2) work before the fixed O(dp) matrix reader. Faster solvers, diagonal approximations or compressed anchors would be specified variants. Resource comparisons must charge the reference activations and extra evaluations rather than counting only model parameters.

**How we would know it addressed B3.** From the same post-update encoder, with the same permitted historical inputs and equivalent computation, a corrected interface should restore a still-valid relation whose information the current representation continues to carry. Gains supported only by interpolation of cached old answers do not establish latent access. Useful checks include held-out old inputs beyond the anchors and controls where the relevant encoder distinction is actually removed; fixed-input anchor agreement alone does not suffice. A linear translation can fail after nonlinear loss or a rank collapse. The operation neither proves nor guarantees that the distinction survived.

### C4 — Construct a rule's scope from positive and contradictory encounters

**Diagnostic target and generated origin.** B1: a useful local regularity becomes a broad relation without preserving the observations that distinguish its valid scope. Origins B1-03, B1-12, B1-14 and B1-24. Unlike C1's retained alternative world descriptions, the move here is constructing an explicit conditional boundary for a particular reusable prediction from actual contrasts.

**Interface and supplied structure.** An experience is (x,a,y,t), with observable descriptor x, action a, observed outcome y and provenance tag t. The tag identifies a declared observation process or intervention type; it is not a hidden truth label. Supply a finite library A of total Boolean tests on x, including any relational tests the learner can evaluate. Conditions may use either polarity of each test; a condition containing the seed uses only the polarity true at that seed. Supply a target function y=F(x) for one proposed rule, a seed encounter on which it held, and a maximum conjunction length k. F, A and their construction are explicit upstream inputs. This operation constructs the rule's scope, not the target relation from nothing. Contrasts cannot reveal distinctions absent from A.

**State.** For each proposed (F,a,t,seed), keep all admitted positive encounters P and contradictory encounters N, and a condition C that is a conjunction of at most k atoms from A. Admitted means same action and provenance tag, with all necessary observed fields present. An unobserved outcome is unknown and enters neither set. The original seed remains in P. Positive and negative refer only to the observed relation y=F(x), not to the reward or desirability of y. Storing all witnesses is the exact candidate; bounded compression is a separate variant.

**Update rule.** On an admitted observation, add it to P if y=F(x), otherwise to N. Enumerate all conjunctions of length at most k that contain the seed and exclude every member of N. Of those, choose the condition covering the largest number of P; break ties by shorter length, then the fixed atom ordering. If none exists, mark the rule unresolved. Do not discard a contradiction, increase k or invent a predicate solely to prevent this outcome.

If no admissible condition exists, first compare the full Boolean test signature of the seed with each negative. If any matches, return `unresolved: observational_aliasing` with the seed and the first such negative: no conjunction of the existing tests can separate that pair. If none matches, return `unresolved: length_bound` with the witness set: separation exists using the full seed-compatible conjunction but not within k. Individual pairs can be separable even when the collection requires a longer conjunction; do not misdiagnose this as a missing observable variable. Only the demonstrated aliasing case requests a new predicate from an upstream description learner. This operation does not implement that learner, increase k or expand the atom library within the instance.

**Use and composition.** The rule licenses F(x) only when C(x) holds and its action/provenance match. If several applicable rules disagree, return unresolved rather than selecting by reward frequency. An ordered sequence of rules can be composed only if each succeeding condition is established by available observations or warranted outputs of preceding rules. Unestablished conditions stay unknown. Passing these checks is empirical compatibility within the description language, not proof that the relation is a causal invariant beyond observed support.

**What changes and what stays unchanged.** This changes the selection and use of a conditional domain, preserving contradictions when defining it. It does not change actions, reset rules, F, the observation channel or the atom library within the instance. Separating provenance stops unlike evidence from silently entering the same support set, but does not infer which sensor was wrong. Where trustworthy labels are unavailable, the unresolved attribution remains an upstream question, as the biology pass requires.

**Discontinuity and cost.** Persist F, atom definitions, C, the seed and witness sets. If an old rule becomes false after a world change, maintaining its condition does not make it valid. With m atoms, k as above and Nw witnesses, exhaustive refinement checks sum_{j=0}^k binomial(m,j) conjunctions, each over Nw witnesses; memory includes all witness descriptors. Approximation or witness eviction changes the candidate and must be named. This enumeration is deliberately specified despite its possible expense.

**How we would know it addressed B1.** With the same observed encounters and proposed F, ordinary selection uses a broadly rewarded relation outside its warranted scope, while preserving its contradictory witnesses leads to a condition that distinguishes the actual reuse cases. Merely refusing more predictions is insufficient: coverage, correct reuse and unresolved decisions must be visible together. If the selected condition is itself a training co-occurrence that fails under the intended rearrangement, the proposed cause remains. A missing observable atom or an untested branch cannot be repaired by asserting a narrower scope with no evidence.

### What the selection commits us to

The four specified operations span three broad causal families: relation selection (C1 and C4), evidence acquisition (C2) and expression/access (C3). Their controlled objects are alternative explanations, selected interactions, conversion into an old query interface, and conditional rule scope; they are not four independently established demands. C1 and C4 both concern relation selection but make different interventions: maintain uncertainty over complete executable descriptions versus construct the applicability of a proposed local prediction. They should be combined only if later evidence requires it. A combined gain would not identify which move mattered.

Other raw families remain visible and unrefuted: literal copies, external environmental memory, exhaustive physical exploration, inverse updates, distributed learners, active reacquisition and schema/predicate discovery. They were not developed in this bounded pass because the selected set spans different causal locations while keeping each operation specifiable. This is convergence after generation, not a finding that the remaining ideas are infeasible or inferior.
