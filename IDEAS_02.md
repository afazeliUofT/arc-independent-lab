## 1. The objective and what this pass changes

The funded objective is to explain failures in acquiring knowledge through interaction with an unfamiliar world, keeping it across discontinuities and recombining it for unfamiliar situations. The approved diagnosis remains the basis for this work. It does not establish that current AI universally fails, that humans or animals solve the full conjunction effortlessly, or that scaling cannot close parts of the gap. Its ranked bottlenecks are investigative priorities with explicit local evidence and unresolved broader attribution. [Approved diagnosis at the current published base](https://github.com/afazeliUofT/arc-independent-lab/blob/53768f9fc6b867834e1ab03b86248b487a19b617/DIAGNOSIS.md), accessed **2026-09-10**; SHA-256 `e3106d737f5b50a688d1d5147a015dc091d2986493848b68d33d61949dfa4cc3`.

The multidisciplinary study is complete within its recorded scope, including recent AI, neuroscience, wider biology, historical methods and the instrumented reproduction. It is not an exhaustive systematic review. The first ideation pass was approved, and the independently reviewed first novelty audit cleared no candidate as a new mechanism. That is a negative novelty outcome, not an efficacy failure. Its separate dispositions and source limits remain in [the published synthesis](https://github.com/afazeliUofT/arc-independent-lab/blob/53768f9fc6b867834e1ab03b86248b487a19b617/reports/P3_DISPOSITION_SYNTHESIS_033.md), accessed **2026-09-10**. The original verdict has SHA-256 `52d533604e9623ae4e5744bd5c81a41b4efac7f41d9a1f7c46c0f76ad31a1f72` and remains unchanged.

The first developed operations mostly received the alternatives, predicates, experiment models or reading interface they manipulated. This pass asks how the learner could construct those useful structures from its own permitted encounters. It specifies five different acquired objects: historical predicates, substitution roles, experimental recipes, evidence-restoring procedures and reading programs. This is a focused generative response to the audit; it does not prove that all five are useful or historically new.

The order was preserved: full raw generation with novelty and feasibility screening OFF; then clustering and selection by diagnostic relevance and diversity; then exact operations; then nearest-method flags. The next primary-method screen has not been performed in this deliverable. The source inputs and their original reading dates are in `evidence/P2_SECOND_GENERATION_INPUT_034.md`.

# Raw second population B1 — forming and revising descriptions

2026-09-10. Generated first, after `P2_SECOND_GENERATION_INPUT_034.md`. Novelty and feasibility screening OFF; no clustering or candidate selection. Each entry attacks description formation or revision within diagnosis priority B1. Origin tags refer to the input record and are prompts, not evidence. These are rough hypotheses, including intentionally excessive ones.

**R2-B1-01 — Give an unexplained difference a place before giving it a meaning. [History; inversion]** When two encounters treated as equivalent yield different continuations, create an unresolved distinction attached to those encounters, without immediately naming a hidden variable. Grow candidate observable tests from their earlier histories and bind the distinction only when a test predicts its recurrence. This could let explanatory structure develop from a recorded obligation to distinguish, instead of requiring the correct predicate before the contradiction can be represented.

**R2-B1-02 — Turn an experiment into a word. [History; orthodoxy violation]** Treat a repeatable action-and-observation procedure as a provisional descriptive primitive: an encounter has a property if that procedure produces a stated contrast. Compose such procedures to describe subsequent encounters, preserving that the act of describing can change the world. Vocabulary would then acquire an operational meaning from interaction rather than inherit an evaluator's feature label.

**R2-B1-03 — Promote a history fragment into a state variable. [History]** After a failed prediction, search stored prefixes for a short event pattern that separates otherwise similar encounters. Add the pattern's running status to the learner's state and revisit predictions previously made without it. A hidden switch or delayed effect could become expressible as remembered history before the learner knows what physical entity carries it.

**R2-B1-04 — Discover boundaries where actions stop commuting. [Combination; orthodoxy violation]** Compare the outcomes of action sequences in different orders and build descriptive units around repeatable order effects. Join or separate units according to where those effects appear and disappear under further encounters. This could discover interactions and dependencies without starting with a complete object decomposition.

**R2-B1-05 — Learn what belongs together by trying to replace it. [History; combination]** Exchange parts of previously successful action sequences between encounters and retain the smallest connected pieces whose replacement preserves an observable consequence. Let those pieces become candidate roles or operations in the description language. Failed substitutions would supply new boundaries for recombination instead of merely lowering a whole-episode score.

**R2-B1-06 — Name an absence by how it can be undone. [Biology—evidence access; inversion]** When a previously visible signal disappears, describe its absence through the learner's attempts to restore the opportunity to observe it. Group absences by restoration behavior rather than by identical blank observations. This could create a distinction between different missing-evidence conditions before any of them has a named cause.

**R2-B1-07 — Let successful actions become candidate causes of ignorance. [Biology—evidence access]** Add descriptions of the learner's own accumulated products and interventions when they repeatedly precede a loss of observable variation. Re-express earlier claims conditionally on those self-produced conditions and seek encounters that separate them. The description language could thereby acquire variables about how evidence is obtained, not only about the apparent objects being predicted.

**R2-B1-08 — Learn a relation that survives competing calibrations. [Biology—feedback attribution; combination]** Fit different candidate interpretations of a changed sensor stream and search for action-outcome comparisons on which they still agree. Promote those comparisons into provisional descriptive primitives while retaining their calibration disagreement. A usable relation could emerge before absolute sensory meaning is settled.

**R2-B1-09 — Invent temporal units from recurrent causal delays. [History; orthodoxy violation]** Allow the learner to redraw event boundaries when an effect repeatedly follows an earlier intervention rather than the latest action. Turn the intervening sequence into a provisional process with its own start, persistence and completion conditions. This could make delayed interactions expressible without supplying a fixed episode segmentation that already locates the cause.

**R2-B1-10 — Grow an object from a bundle of controllable consequences. [Combination]** Track sets of observable changes that repeatedly travel together across different permitted manipulations, then introduce a provisional common bearer for the bundle. Split or merge that bearer when later manipulations separate or unite the consequences. Object identity would be a revisable explanatory convenience built from effects rather than a permanently assigned sensory token.

**R2-B1-11 — Let magnitude appear only when order ceases to suffice. [History; inversion]** Begin with coarse descriptions such as different, earlier and reversible. Add counts, distances or continuous quantities only at recurring failures that those coarse distinctions cannot resolve, choosing constructions from the actual interaction trace. This reverses the assumption that a rich numerical state must precede learning the relations that make a quantity useful.

**R2-B1-12 — Invent a conserved quantity out of closed excursions. [History; orthodoxy violation]** Search for combinations of observed changes that cancel when the learner returns through a successful action cycle. Use the recurring cancellation to propose a quantity or balance relation, then carry it into new compositions. The learner could acquire a constraint that organizes unfamiliar transitions without first receiving the world's preferred variables.

**R2-B1-13 — Ask several descriptions to repair the same failure. [Combination]** Maintain separate description builders that extend temporal memory, partition observations, introduce relational roles or change the observation account. Give each the same failed prediction and retain the distinct repairs it constructs. Competition would occur over explanations the learner can actually produce, rather than only over hypotheses supplied complete at initialization.

**R2-B1-14 — Make the grammar itself learn from its failed searches. [Orthodoxy violation]** Record which fragments recur in partially successful explanations even when no complete explanation fits. Promote useful fragments into new construction operators and allow later explanations to use them as primitives. This might reduce repeated rediscovery and let the learner's language change as its encounters expose recurring explanatory work.

**R2-B1-15 — Store a relation between representations before choosing either one. [History; combination]** When two description systems support overlapping predictions, learn a partial translation from the encounters where they meet and preserve the unmapped portions. Let that translation create new shared roles only where further use warrants them. Recombination could begin through a learned correspondence without an early irreversible choice of a single world description.

**R2-B1-16 — Make contradiction enlarge a relation's arguments. [History]** Start with a relation of few arguments; when its use fails systematically, search the local encounter history for another participant, ordering or condition that explains the failure. Extend the relation's argument structure and retain how the new argument was discovered. This could grow relational complexity from concrete failures rather than require all useful arities and roles to be specified beforehand.

**R2-B1-17 — Make descriptions serve questions that disagree. [Inversion; combination]** Let different self-generated questions produce their own local partitions of the same encounters, then learn where one partition refines or conflicts with another. Preserve a way to move between them instead of requiring one representation to be sufficient for every possible demand. An agent might learn a usable family of descriptions from actual differences in informational need.

**R2-B1-18 — Develop a vocabulary through restricted action. [Orthodoxy violation]** Begin with a deliberately restricted subset of the available actions, learn descriptions of what can be changed within it, and reopen additional actions gradually. Revisit the earlier vocabulary whenever an added action separates cases it had merged. Description formation would acquire an ordered history of revisions rather than start with all degrees of freedom competing at once.

**R2-B1-19 — Delete a familiar name to expose the evidence that sustained it. [Inversion]** Temporarily remove a learned symbolic label while preserving the encounters and procedures associated with its use. Ask the learner to reconstruct a usable distinction under another description and retain any structure that the old name had concealed. This could make names replaceable and reveal when apparent knowledge consists only of successful label reuse.

**R2-B1-20 — Keep every sensory fragment as its own theory. [Deliberate extreme]** Create a separate explanatory process for every encountered fragment and let processes communicate only through proposed predictions about one another. Never merge their private descriptions, even when they appear redundant. The excessive decentralization could reveal a route by which a shared relation emerges through communication instead of a central predesigned ontology.

**R2-B1-21 — Rebuild the entire language after every surprise. [Deliberate extreme; inversion]** On each unexpected event, discard the current descriptive primitives and reconstruct a language from the full preserved interaction history. Require old queries to be translated into the rebuilt language as part of reconstruction. This gives revision maximum freedom and makes the price of protecting an initially mistaken vocabulary explicit.

**R2-B1-22 — Permit a new hidden cause for every observation. [Deliberate extreme]** Explain every new encounter with its own latent cause and only later demand that causes be combined to answer a fresh question. Let the pressure of that question determine which invented causes can share an operational role. This deliberately postpones compression so far that relation discovery happens at reuse rather than acquisition.

**R2-B1-23 — Refuse hidden causes altogether. [Deliberate extreme; inversion]** Represent only executable transformations between observed histories and the tests that distinguish their continuations. Build higher-level descriptions by composing those transformations, with no latent object or causal entity permitted. This asks how far useful unfamiliar-world knowledge can develop without solving the naming problem it is often assumed to require.

**R2-B1-24 — Let two incompatible languages teach each other new experiments. [Combination; history]** Have two learners describe the same encounters in deliberately different primitive languages, and exchange only executable questions and observed answers. When one language can express a reproducible distinction the other cannot, let the latter extend itself to account for the exchanged procedure. New descriptive capacity could arise from an interaction between incompatible accounts rather than a teacher supplying the desired representation.


# Raw second population B2 — obtaining evidence before knowing what matters

2026-09-10. Generated after B1 was saved in `P2_SECOND_B1_SAVED_034.json`. Novelty and feasibility screening remain OFF; no clustering or selection. Each entry changes how a learner obtains evidence while its descriptive or predictive account is incomplete. Origin tags refer to `P2_SECOND_GENERATION_INPUT_034.md`; all entries are hypotheses.

**R2-B2-01 — Mutate a successful procedure at its seams. [History; inversion]** Take an action sequence that already produces a reproducible effect and change, omit, duplicate or swap one of its steps. Keep the resulting contrasts even if the learner cannot explain them yet. Success becomes a source of experimental structure, allowing new dependencies to be exposed without an advance model of which variation is informative.

**R2-B2-02 — Repeat the same ending after different beginnings. [Combination]** Replay a common action suffix after deliberately varied prefixes and record whether its consequences depend on the route taken. Extend the prefix when an apparently identical current observation hides different outcomes. This could acquire evidence for latent state or delayed effects before the learner can name the missing state variable.

**R2-B2-03 — Collect operations that reopen access. [Orthodoxy violation]** Give exploratory credit to a sequence that restores several previously lost opportunities for action or observation, even when it teaches no immediate world fact. Store how to reach that sequence's starting conditions. A repertoire of recoverable encounters could preserve the ability to ask useful questions after those questions become expressible.

**R2-B2-04 — Sweep the strength of an intervention without predicting the curve. [History]** Vary an available action's duration, magnitude or repetition across an expanding range and record qualitative changes in the observed response. Concentrate later variation around changes in that empirical pattern rather than around uncertainty in a fitted model. New regimes could become visible before the learner possesses hypotheses capable of forecasting them.

**R2-B2-05 — Dither the conditions under which success hides evidence. [Biology—evidence access]** Introduce small reversible variations in a successful behavior and track which observation opportunities return as its effect weakens or changes. Retain the direction of those exposure changes as knowledge about the observation process. This could reveal distinctions systematically absent under the fully effective policy without requiring a known catalogue of the hidden distinctions.

**R2-B2-06 — Move the measurement while repeating the action. [Biology—feedback attribution; combination]** Repeat a behavior while varying available sensing time, position or reporting transformation, and separately repeat a measurement arrangement across changed behaviors. Preserve the crossed observations before fitting an attribution model. The acquired contrasts could later support a distinction between action effects and observation effects that neither side alone makes expressible.

**R2-B2-07 — Search for the smallest way to break reproducibility. [Inversion]** Starting from an empirically repeatable encounter, vary one accessible preparation detail at a time until the outcome changes. Then carry that detail into later experiments as a provisional control variable. The question is initially what disrupts a reliable procedure, rather than which of a supplied list of theories it disproves.

**R2-B2-08 — Keep the unexplained trace, change the route to it. [History; combination]** Return to an unexplained event through different learned action routes and collect what remains common across its occurrences. Routes can be generated by recombining the learner's own procedure fragments without predicting the event's cause. This could distinguish an encounter-specific regularity from an accidental feature of the original acquisition path.

**R2-B2-09 — Explore what stops changing. [Inversion]** After a stretch of varied behavior produces the same observations, deliberately change the kind or timescale of intervention rather than simply increasing its amount. Store the observation plateau and the first procedure that escapes it. The plateau can organize inquiry even when a confident predictor assigns no uncertainty to the concealed difference.

**R2-B2-10 — Learn experimental habits from actual surprises they unlock. [Orthodoxy violation]** Give families of experiment mutations credit when their collected observations later force useful revisions of the description language. Retain that delayed credit separately from immediate prediction error or reward. The learner could acquire ways of looking that repeatedly reveal omitted structure without computing information gain inside a model that cannot yet represent it.

**R2-B2-11 — Borrow an experiment from an analogous failure. [History; combination]** Store unresolved encounter patterns together with the procedures that later made them intelligible. When a new failure has a partially matching action-observation pattern, translate and try the earlier procedure through the current interface. Analogy would propose evidence-gathering actions before supplying an explanation of the new world.

**R2-B2-12 — Use strangers to the current policy. [Combination]** Maintain action generators trained for deliberately different simple objectives, and periodically let one operate from a state reached by another. Collect the consequences of the mismatched policy and starting condition, including failed transitions. This could expose interactions excluded by each policy's familiar trajectory without requiring a joint model in advance.

**R2-B2-13 — Give an observation two unrelated witnesses. [Biology—feedback attribution; orthodoxy violation]** Seek different accessible action or sensing routes that bear on the same suspected consequence, and compare their agreement over changing conditions. Search the routes from observed co-variation before assuming either is a calibrated reference. Partial agreement could create evidence about the reliability of the observation relation itself.

**R2-B2-14 — Preserve a sample of missed opportunities. [Biology—evidence access; combination]** At selected decisions, record feasible but unused actions and the conditions that made them available; later revisit some of those conditions and take the neglected branch. Prioritize opportunities that disappear under increasing competence. This turns omissions in the learner's own action history into experimental material without claiming that an unexecuted branch already has a known outcome.

**R2-B2-15 — Change who produces an effect. [History; orthodoxy violation]** Where the interface permits it, produce a similar observable consequence through different controllable participants or action sources. Then test which subsequent changes follow the consequence and which follow its producer. These substitutions could expose causal roles before the learner has a settled account of the entities occupying them.

**R2-B2-16 — Let experiments lengthen after their apparent ending. [Inversion]** Continue observing and occasionally acting after the event that ordinarily ends a successful trial. Lengthen or rearrange the continuation when effects recur after delays. This could reveal unfinished processes that the current episode boundary hides, without first positing which delayed variable should exist.

**R2-B2-17 — Collect the same encounter at different ages of the learner's own products. [Biology—evidence access]** Revisit a procedure soon after, and long after, the learner establishes an external arrangement or repeated intervention pattern. Compare what becomes observable as those products persist, accumulate or decay. Such encounters could expose evidence-access dynamics that a single time slice mistakes for fixed properties of the world.

**R2-B2-18 — Let a vocabulary gap request a contrast, not an explanation. [Combination]** Allow the description learner to report only that two recorded encounters still need separating, without providing alternative world models. Have an explorer try mutations of the encounters' action histories and return the resulting paired traces. This couples representation growth to interaction while permitting the question to precede an adequate explanatory language.

**R2-B2-19 — Manufacture controlled mess. [Deliberate extreme; orthodoxy violation]** Combine many available actions in dense, irregular sequences and retain the full timing record, even when individual effects cannot initially be separated. Use later sparse interventions to interpret recurring fragments of the resulting traces. The deliberately tangled exposure could reveal interactions that isolated primitive tests never bring into view.

**R2-B2-20 — Repeat a single boring action for the whole acquisition period. [Deliberate extreme; inversion]** Choose one action that appears to have no immediate effect and repeat it without switching to novelty-seeking behavior. Observe accumulation, adaptation, delayed transitions and disappearance of evidence under that prolonged exposure. This extreme could expose a process that a policy rewarding rapid observable change continually abandons too early.

**R2-B2-21 — Visit every action sequence before explaining any of them. [Deliberate extreme; history]** Dovetail execution across progressively longer action sequences and store complete observations without learning an explanatory model during collection. Only a later stage may turn those encounters into variables or theories. This removes model adequacy from the acquisition decision entirely and makes exhaustive exposure a visible extreme of the design space.

**R2-B2-22 — Undo every success before using it again. [Deliberate extreme; inversion]** After obtaining a desired effect, devote the next interaction to reversing or disrupting it, then reconstruct it through a different sequence. Preserve the paired construction and destruction traces. This could turn competence into repeated evidence about conditions of possibility rather than a reason to stop encountering them.

**R2-B2-23 — Learn which experiment sequences create new experiments. [History; combination]** Treat a procedure as useful when its result makes an additional family of interventions possible, even if those interventions have no current explanatory purpose. Build longer exploratory plans by chaining these empirically discovered changes in controllability. Knowledge acquisition could advance by expanding the available questions rather than optimizing answers inside a fixed experiment set.

**R2-B2-24 — Teach inquiry by exchanging failures of description. [Combination]** Let learners with different sensory encodings exchange pairs of encounters each encoding cannot distinguish, together with procedures that reached them. Each learner searches its own interface for an observable contrast and returns an executable test if it finds one. The collaboration could expose missing evidence without one learner supplying the other's hidden variables or final answer.


# Raw second population B3 — discovering a usable reading operation

2026-09-10. Generated after B2 was saved in `P2_SECOND_B2_SAVED_034.json`. Novelty and feasibility screening remain OFF; no clustering or selection. Each entry addresses access to acquired distinctions across a changing state or interface, the third diagnostic priority. Origin tags refer to `P2_SECOND_GENERATION_INPUT_034.md`; all entries are hypotheses. A retained example, response, program, update trace or world arrangement is a proposed history-bearing channel, not free evidence.

**R2-B3-01 — Learn the reader on changes whose effects the learner can observe. [Diagnostic; combination]** Before an actual discontinuity, make trial changes to copies of the learner's own state and search for reading programs that preserve selected self-generated responses. Train the search procedure across those observed changes, then apply it to a later changed state using only the declared surviving information. This could teach a process for finding a reader instead of supplying one fixed geometric formula.

**R2-B3-02 — Discover a distinction's sensitivity by perturbing the carrier. [Diagnostic; orthodoxy violation]** Probe accessible components of current state with reversible trial perturbations and measure how candidate readings change. Search for combinations of readings with recurring different sensitivity patterns, retaining how those combinations were found. An agent might learn where usable distinctions reside through its own experiments on state rather than an analyst's coordinate decomposition.

**R2-B3-03 — Preserve relations among answers as a reader-selection problem. [History; combination]** During acquisition, record self-observed relations among responses to multiple queries, such as equality, ordering or composition, and the procedures that produced them. After change, search reading programs whose joint responses satisfy that retained structure. The memory target becomes a network of constraints that helps identify an access route, with the constraint record itself counted as retained knowledge.

**R2-B3-04 — Ground a reader through a newly accessible consequence. [Biology—feedback attribution; combination]** Let competing readings of retained state propose different actions in a currently accessible situation. Use the newly observed consequence to update the correspondence between a reading and its claimed meaning. This could reacquire semantic grounding for an old carrier while making the additional interaction part of the recovery operation.

**R2-B3-05 — Let a failed query generate its own intermediate questions. [History]** Decompose an unsuccessful query into candidate subquestions that can be asked through the current interface, and search for combinations of their responses that predict a permitted check on the original query. Keep successful decompositions as learned reading programs. Access would grow from reconstructing an executable route, rather than assuming an old query must reach one unchanged internal location.

**R2-B3-06 — Observe an update through many self-chosen windows. [Diagnostic; combination]** Before and after selected learning changes, probe the state through a varied set of available response procedures and preserve a compact description of their co-change. Later reader search can use those observed patterns to hypothesize which changes disturbed a requested distinction. The learned object is an empirical account of update effects, with its probes and retained traces charged as memory and computation.

**R2-B3-07 — Grow a reading language from successful recoveries. [History; orthodoxy violation]** Search primitive compositions over the learner's declared state operations whenever an access problem occurs. When a composition succeeds under permitted checks, promote reusable fragments into the language used by later searches. This could make decoder discovery accumulate procedural knowledge instead of restarting from an externally supplied decoder family on every occasion.

**R2-B3-08 — Let readers inherit a lineage of changes. [Combination]** Associate a learned reading program with the sequence of representation revisions under which it was constructed, then search local program edits when another revision arrives. Reuse edits that previously repaired similar observed disruptions. The agent could learn how access transformations compose without freezing the original representation or receiving an exact inverse for each update.

**R2-B3-09 — Break the reader on purpose while the lesson is still accessible. [Inversion; diagnostic]** Deliberately corrupt or remove an access route during acquisition while the relevant encounter can still be revisited. Learn a repair procedure from the difference between failure, trial repair and the permitted fresh observation. Repeated recoveries could train reader discovery under known learning opportunities before it is needed after an irreversible gap.

**R2-B3-10 — Use disagreement to choose what to calibrate. [Combination]** Generate several reading programs from the current state and identify where their implications differ in situations the learner can still inspect. Spend new interactions on those points and retain any unresolved readings when the checks do not separate them. The operation would learn both a reader and when there is enough evidence to invoke it.

**R2-B3-11 — Carry a recipe for reconstructing a question's meaning. [History; inversion]** Store the procedure by which a query acquired its meaning, including the learner's actions and comparisons, rather than only its old symbolic name. After a representation change, reinterpret the procedure through current capabilities and search for a compatible reading. This could make the semantics of a query renewable through interaction instead of permanently attached to an index.

**R2-B3-12 — Learn access through a loop of translations. [History; combination]** Build tentative translations among several internally available descriptions and test whether a query transformed around a loop returns the same operational consequences. Use failures of closure to guide edits to particular translations. Agreement across the loop could constrain reader construction while leaving its connection to the world available for separate grounding.

**R2-B3-13 — Make the learner choose a memory that teaches its future reader. [Orthodoxy violation]** During acquisition, let simulated future readers attempt recovery under several self-generated state changes. Preserve the pieces of evidence or procedure that most change those readers' ability to reconstruct usable access. Memory selection would target learnability of a later reading operation, with all retained material treated as part of the channel.

**R2-B3-14 — Preserve the experiment that exposes a distinction, then relearn its internal address. [Biology—evidence access; combination]** Keep an executable procedure that once made a distinction observable and use it after change to compare new observations with candidate expressions in retained state. Search for the expression whose behavior tracks the renewed contrast. The proposal couples an external opportunity for evidence to discovery of an internal access route, rather than assuming either remains calibrated.

**R2-B3-15 — Let the same acquired relation move between different carriers. [Orthodoxy violation]** Periodically transfer a relation among models, symbolic records and executable procedures using the learner's own permitted checks, and preserve how successful transfers were recognized. Use the resulting transfer history to train reader discovery after an unplanned carrier change. The variation could make access learning depend on operational consequences instead of one privileged storage format.

**R2-B3-16 — Read through what the state is ready to learn next. [Diagnostic; inversion]** On temporary copies, apply controlled hypothetical learning signals and observe the resulting changes in behavior. Search for old distinctions expressed in differential update responses rather than in the ordinary prediction itself. A learned reading procedure could exploit information in the state's susceptibility to change, with each trial, signal and temporary copy accounted for.

**R2-B3-17 — Construct a reader by matching patterns of failure. [History; combination]** Save descriptions of how an established relation failed to be expressed during earlier repair episodes, alongside the edits that restored permitted use. Match a current failure pattern to those cases and adapt a prior repair program. Access could become a learned diagnostic skill without treating the earlier repair as a universally correct decoder.

**R2-B3-18 — Make every read prove its route on a nearby task. [Orthodoxy violation]** Before relying on a candidate reader, use it on a related question whose consequence the learner can currently check. Let success, failure and mismatch between the two questions update when the reader is invoked. This adds a learned applicability condition to access, so locating a plausible value does not automatically authorize its use everywhere.

**R2-B3-19 — Keep an autobiography of every update. [Deliberate extreme]** Preserve every learning input, every intermediate state and every response ever produced, then search that complete record for a reconstruction of the requested reading operation. Allow the reconstruction to generate a smaller program only after it works through the full history. This maximal extra channel exposes how far access can be guaranteed by retaining the process that created the knowledge.

**R2-B3-20 — Forget every answer but remember how you checked it. [Deliberate extreme; inversion]** Remove direct stored responses and keep only the learner's checking procedures, their origins and any non-answer state needed to execute them. At reuse, search for readings whose consequences survive those procedures, supplementing them with new interaction where permitted. This extreme asks how much useful access can be rebuilt from the means of warranting an answer rather than an archive of answers.

**R2-B3-21 — Replace the reader with an entire society of guesses. [Deliberate extreme]** Enumerate arbitrarily many reading programs over current state and let each drive its own temporary simulated or permitted real interaction. Keep their full disagreements and allow a task-specific process to recruit a coalition of compatible readings. This removes a unique privileged decoder at the cost of an intentionally enormous access process.

**R2-B3-22 — Abandon internal recovery and ask the world again. [Deliberate extreme; inversion]** Treat every failed old query as a demand for fresh acquisition, retaining only procedures for revisiting the relevant conditions. Reconstruct the answer through current interaction and use the result to build a new access route. This places reacquisition at the extreme opposite of latent decoding and makes the survival of useful procedures the proposed continuity channel.

**R2-B3-23 — Make new descriptions carry unresolved old questions. [Combination]** When the learner revises its world vocabulary, translate each still-relevant old question into a set of executable candidate interpretations in the new vocabulary. Let later use and observations separate those interpretations instead of assigning one translation immediately. The reader would co-develop with description revision while preserving uncertainty about what an old request now means.

**R2-B3-24 — Learn when to stop repairing access and reopen acquisition. [Diagnostic; combination]** Train a controller on episodes where trial readers, retained evidence and new encounters lead to different patterns of successful use. Let it choose between continued internal search and a concrete new experiment, retaining the reason for the choice. This could join decoder discovery to the higher-priority evidence problem without assuming that every old-task failure is recoverable from surviving state alone.

The important distinction is between acquiring a predicate, acquiring a relation of substitution, acquiring an experiment and learning when evidence is accessible. They can share a history but change different parts of the computational problem. A decoder is a further, lower-priority operation; it cannot establish that a useful world relation was acquired in the first place.

Several proposals about preserving multiple languages, learning translations and carrying unresolved questions overlap these moves. I retain them as available alternatives and specification constraints, rather than compressing them into a vaguely specified whole architecture. The extreme ideas also remain: full histories, exhaustive exposure, total language rebuilding and complete reacquisition were not discarded for expense or familiarity.

## 4. The five directions selected for development

| Operation | Why develop it | Actual acquired object | Principal limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| N1: historical predicate construction | Directly attacks a merged description despite discriminating history already being available. | Tests selected from the learner's own transition conflicts. | Supplied tokenization and grammar; empirical separation can overfit. |
| N2: context-qualified substitution | Makes recombination depend on acquired replaceability, not supplied module roles. | Empirical roles and explicitly fallible transfer conjectures among recipe fragments. | Finite tested contexts do not imply universal interchangeability. |
| N3: growing experiment library | Acquires experiments before an adequate model can value them. | Executed recipes, useful fragments and priorities updated by subsequent predictive use. | Local credit may reward extra fitting or miss important future demands. |
| N4: restoring observable contrast | Addresses the learner's own effect on access to evidence. | Actual recipes and conditions under which a lost observable contrast returns. | A restored signal need not expose a useful hidden distinction. |
| N5: learned reading program | Targets the gap between surviving information and a reader the learner can obtain. | A source-trained expression for reading current state. | Source responses, grammar and query encoding are additional channels and priors. |

The selection is for diagnostic relevance and diversity of underlying move. It is not a novelty ranking or a feasibility ranking. N1 and N2 address the first diagnostic priority; N3 and N4 address acquisition of evidence; N5 retains the lower-priority access question. Origins are given in each specification, and none of the other raw entries is retired.

The strongest bridge to the full objective is still an empirical question. N4 is especially weak until the restored contrast changes a later useful decision. N5 learns an expression; its reference cache does not constitute a newly learned search algorithm. The resettable and finite-input assumptions in the specifications are substantial. They define inspectable reference operations, not an assertion that those conditions cover the entire funded category.

# Second-pass operations

## 0. What is acquired, and what is supplied

The five operations acquire different objects: historical predicates (N1), substitution relations (N2), experimental recipes and their search priorities (N3), procedures for reopening observable contrasts (N4), and reading programs (N5). Supplying a generative syntax is unavoidable; supplying the finished useful distinction is not. We expose the syntax and the actual encounter-dependent update in each case. None discovers arbitrary semantics without priors.

N1–N4 first receive a deliberately explicit interface: observations are fixed-length tuples of finite categorical tokens; actions are a finite ordered set of primitive commands. Tokenization, field order, available actions, episode markers and their costs are supplied. No hidden state, object roles, correct predicates, causal graph, completed world-model family or future evaluation questions are supplied. This does not solve perception from images or discover physical action primitives. It allows learning new predicates, roles and **compositions** of the supplied primitives. The interface bias remains material.

Valid configurations use nonempty token/action alphabets, a positive observation-tuple length, positive `L`, positive N2 panel size `J`, nonnegative finite resource ceilings, and finite integer grammar/history/feature bounds in their stated domains. `W,K,K_read` may be zero; syntax-size bounds and `Q_max` must be positive. Calls are serialized: complete or explicitly suspend one state update before admitting another. An operation that cannot finish a required query-support check within its allowance returns `COMPUTATION_INCOMPLETE`, not a stale supported answer.

N2–N4 additionally require a stationary deterministic **resettable block**: a permitted `RESET` command restores the same unknown initial physical state within that block. This is a strong declared environmental affordance, not something inferred from similar observations. RESET costs an interaction and its result is logged. Blocks may have different unknown dynamics, but all local empirical effect tables carry a block identifier and are cleared when a new block begins. The identifier declares a change boundary, not the world's parameters. Procedure syntax and proposed transferable relations may survive, with their original block provenance. If this reset contract is absent, the reference operations return `UNSUPPORTED_INTERFACE`; observational return is not substituted for a physical reset. Extending the operation to stochastic, drifting or nonresettable worlds is later work.

### Shared execution and order

A history contains only completed observations, issued actions and declared boundaries. Each transition is `(history_before, action, observation_after)`; an evaluator's latent-state trace is outside learner state. A recipe is an ordered finite word of primitive actions. Its name is its serialized word, not an evaluator-assigned semantic label. Concatenation is literal execution, never a free call. Executing `Trial(w)` means RESET and then every action in `w`, logging all intermediate observations and costs. An endpoint is the entire final observation tuple. No hidden-state equality is inferred from endpoint equality. A trial ends in the physical state it reached; the next trial pays for its own reset.

RESET is a boundary event in the log, not a primitive prediction action in N1. Its returned observation starts the next before-history; only ordinary primitive-action transitions enter N1's transition table. RESET remains charged and recorded even though it is not a supervised transition. On a declared block change, archive old block-specific tables and their provenance, then start new active empirical tables; archived bytes still count as retained memory. N1 may retain its predicate syntax as proposed reusable functions, but its active transition/count table starts empty in a new block. Reads of old empirical records must identify their old block explicitly. No old effect is relabeled as an observation from the new block.

A configuration fixes maximum recipe length `L`, repetition count `m >= 2`, maximum total environment calls `B_env`, maximum primitive computation operations `B_comp`, and maximum retained bytes `B_mem`. These are operation parameters and resource ceilings, not result thresholds. A valid word must fit `L`. A new full trial starts only if its reset plus actions fit the remaining environment allowance. On an unexpected interruption, retain the partial trace as `INCOMPLETE`; it supplies individual completed transitions to N1 but no endpoint to a completed-trial table. Stop at a bound without deleting old evidence. Serialization orders actions and tokens by their supplied index, words by length then lexicographically, and records by acquisition index. These orders break every unspecified tie below.

Because observations have a declared finite maximum serialized size, reserve storage for the next action record and response before issuing that action. Reserve memory separately before adding library entries, search snapshots or tables. If a reservation cannot fit, stop with `MEMORY_LIMIT` while preserving completed evidence; never issue an action and then silently drop its response to meet the cap. Computation is counted in the operations named below and arithmetic bit costs; implementations must declare their accounting convention before measurement.

`Effect(w)` is `UNTESTED` until `m` completed trials in the same block exist; it is `REPEATABLE(y)` if all recorded endpoints equal `y`, otherwise `VARIABLE(all_endpoints)`. A later differing endpoint revokes repeatability. It is an empirical status, not a probability-one claim or certification of the reset contract. A VARIABLE outcome suspends the affected deterministic inference; retain it rather than average away the contradiction. Invalid interfaces, exhausted bounds, untested cases and contradictory observations have different status values.

An agent-memory discontinuity is separate from physical RESET. Persistent state is exactly the state listed for the operation, including histories, libraries, search cursors and calibration data. A volatile reset clears only call stacks and incomplete local computations; it does not silently erase those persistent objects. A forced deletion of any persistent field creates an explicitly different ablation. No selected operation has a free history, feature, label, experiment or decoder oracle.

N1 consumes an externally specified stream and changes its description. N2 and N4 each expose their own deterministic next-trial scheduler below. N3 constructs its own stream and calls the fully specified N1 as its description learner; those calls and copies count. N5 uses a different, declared learning-state interface. These are component interventions, **not an already specified universal joint controller**. A later combination must specify the interleaving and shared-state semantics anew.

## N1. Construct historical predicates from unresolved transitions

**Origins and target.** R2-B1-01/03/09/16 and R2-B2-02/18. B1: a learner's current description merges encounters with observably different continuations. The learned object is an executable test of the permitted history, not a selected member of a supplied list of complete worlds.

**State and inputs.** Persistent transition table `T`; ordered selected predicate list `Phi`; predicate syntax inventory; unresolved transition-pair records; feature-construction log; and counts used for prediction. Initially `T` and `Phi` are empty. Each completed transition appends to `T`. Its before-history ends at the current observation, and lags never cross an episode marker.

**Supplied grammar.** Configuration fixes history limit `W`, maximum Boolean syntax-tree size `S`, and feature limit `K`. Leaves are equality tests between two same-type history terms, or a term and a same-type constant token. Terms are observation field `j` at lag `k` for `0 <= k <= W`, or an action at lag `k` for `1 <= k <= W`. Only tokens already observed by the learner may become constants. Missing history has a dedicated `MISSING` value that compares equal only to itself; action and observation types are distinct. Internal operators are ordered `NOT`, `AND`, `OR`, with ordinary Boolean semantics and fixed token-index serialization. Enumerate every well-typed expression of size at most `S`, deduplicating identical serialized trees, in size/serialization order. These choices supply representational biases, but no named world variable or correct separating test.

For a before-history ending in `o_t`, observation lag zero is `o_t`; action lag one is the already issued action `a_(t-1)` that produced it. The action now being predicted is the separate key argument `a_t` and is not a past-history term. A lag extending before the last RESET or block boundary is MISSING.

**Update.** The key for a transition is `(current_observation, Phi(history_before), action)`. A conflicting pair has equal keys and unequal next observations. For each not-yet-selected candidate predicate `p`, compute `gain(p)` as the number of currently conflicting unordered pairs for which its Boolean outputs differ. Choose the largest strictly positive gain, then smallest syntax size, then serialization order. Append that predicate to `Phi`, recompute all keys and repeat until no positive-gain predicate exists, `K` predicates have been selected, or the computation bound prevents a complete candidate pass. A partially evaluated pass makes no selection; its unfinished cursor can be resumed. Preserve every conflicting pair still merged, along with whether the search was complete, grammar-limited, feature-limited or interrupted. Existing predicates and transitions are not removed when a new one is added.

After every completed selection pass, rebuild a count table for all retained transitions under the new key. `Predict(history,a)` returns the selected feature vector, observed outcome counts and a distribution `(count(y)+1)/(n+|O|)` over the declared finite observation space `O`. For an unseen key this is uniform. It additionally reports `UNSEEN_KEY`, `OBSERVED_SINGLE_OUTCOME` or `OBSERVED_CONFLICT`; these empirical labels never mean identified world truth. Predictions issued before a transition arrives are logged before its observation is added.

Every search cursor is tagged with the exact transition-table version, predicate-list version and constant-token inventory. Resume it only while these match; otherwise discard the stale cursor, retain its spent-compute record and begin a new pass. Counts for the currently selected predicates must reflect all completed active-block transitions even when feature search is interrupted. If the computation allowance cannot finish the required count rebuild, prediction returns `COMPUTATION_INCOMPLETE` rather than using stale counts. N3 cannot score such a prediction as a numeric probability.

**What is learned and unchanged.** Which historical tests become state variables changes in response to the learner's own observed conflicts. The primitive action set, observation serialization, grammar operators and existing history bytes stay fixed. More Boolean tests of a fully retained history are not new external information. This operation tries to make already obtained distinctions usable to the predictor. It does not itself obtain an experiment, infer a latent entity, or prove that the selected distinction is causal.

**Cost and limits.** With `n` transitions, `P` candidate trees, maximum evaluation size `S`, and at most `K` additions, a direct complete update costs `O(K P n^2 S)` operations plus count rebuilding; transition histories, predicate values, expressions and pair witnesses all count as memory. An indexed implementation must preserve the same selected order. Grammar/feature/resource exhaustion is not evidence of observational non-identifiability. Histories identical under all permitted observations may genuinely remain unresolved; a split that fits past noise may also be wrong. No stochastic extension is supplied here.

**How the diagnosis would be tested later.** On equal acquired traces, compare subsequent usable predictions with an otherwise matched fixed-description learner and with an equal-compute unrestricted history reader. Link an improvement to a particular learned predicate and its counterexample provenance, including unobserved compositions that were not used to select it. If the same gain comes from extra replay, a larger history allowance or a supplied test, description acquisition has not been isolated. A new predicate that only separates its training pair has not repaired reuse.

## N2. Learn context-qualified substitution roles

**Origins and target.** R2-B1-04/05/10/12 and R2-B2-15. B1 and the funded recombination demand: learn which **acquired** procedure fragments may replace one another for an observable consequence. This changes reusable relations among fragments, not just the number of flat history features.

**State.** An ordered acquired word library `F`, candidate hole-context list `C`, completed trial table, a versioned context panel, role signatures and failed-substitution witnesses. Each actual executed action word supplies all its nonempty contiguous fragments of length at most `L` to `F`, in acquisition/order order. Every observed word `u f v` supplies the hole context `(u,v)` for each nonempty contiguous fragment `f`; retain empty prefixes and suffixes. Contexts and fragments are acquired from executed words, not annotated by an outside role detector. Initially use one single-action trial per primitive action in order to seed this process.

**Trials and panel.** Configuration fixes panel size `J`. The panel is the first `J` distinct acquired contexts; before it fills, its version changes whenever a context is added. At each call enumerate valid `(context,fragment)` combinations with total length at most `L`, ordered by context acquisition index then fragment acquisition index. Execute the oldest combination lacking `m` completed trials. If none exists, execute the first unexecuted primitive action word of length at most `L` and acquire its fragments/contexts. Stop if neither exists or a bound prevents execution. New trials update the same word effects; identical words reached through different decompositions share their actual trace records. No presumed substitution is counted as an executed trial.

**Role update.** For a panel version, a fragment has a complete signature only when every panel-context composition is valid and has a REPEATABLE endpoint. Its signature is the ordered vector of those endpoints. Fragments with identical complete signatures form an empirical role class. Missing or VARIABLE cells do not become matches; they leave the fragment unclassified in that panel. Store literal signatures and context provenance, not a transitive closure through partially overlapping observations. Distinct endpoint vectors are failed substitutions. A changed panel can split or dissolve a role class; keep the earlier version as historical evidence.

The scheduler covers the full acquired `C × F` set, not just the panel; the panel only defines the role signature. Tag each signature with block, panel and effect-table versions. Before a role-based query, recompute it from the current table if any version changed. Later VARIABLE evidence therefore revokes present support without deleting its historical record.

**Reading and recombination.** A query `(u,v,f,g)` asks whether replacing `f` by `g` would preserve the endpoint of `u f v`. If both words have actual repeatable effects, return those observations and their equality status. Otherwise, if `f` and `g` have the same complete role signature and `u f v` has an actual repeatable effect, return `TRANSFER_CONJECTURE(y)` for `u g v`, accompanied by the supporting panel and untested context. If the source effect is unavailable or the role relation is unsupported, return `UNRESOLVED`. Querying never executes the replacement secretly. The conjecture may be passed to a separate fixed action chooser; any actual execution is logged as new evidence.

First validate that all four query arguments are words over the primitive action set, `f` and `g` are nonempty members of the acquired fragment library, and both compositions fit `L`; otherwise return `INVALID_QUERY`. If either composed word has VARIABLE current-block evidence, return that evidence and `VARIABLE`, ahead of any role conjecture. Only current-block signatures support the reference inference. Old block roles remain archived hypotheses rather than silently becoming current observations.

**What is acquired and what is not.** The role class, candidate decomposition boundaries and context-qualified substitution conjecture come from actual compositions. Input participant names are still supplied tokens; this is not discovery of physical objects. Endpoint-preserving fragments need not preserve intermediate states, costs, safety, future behavior or all effects. The operator offers an explicitly falsifiable extension to a new context, not a theorem that panel equivalence is a congruence of the unknown world.

**Reset and cost.** Word syntax, provenance and old roles may persist across memory discontinuities; empirical tables are block-specific under the shared reset rule. Each context-fragment cell costs `m` full reset-and-word trials. With `c` contexts and `f` fragments, the direct table has up to `cf` cells, each with complete traces; panels require `O(Jf)` endpoint comparisons per rebuild, besides execution and sorting. Invalid long compositions are absent cells, not negative evidence. No generic simulation of the world is supplied.

**Later diagnostic contrast.** Ask about compositions absent from the acquisition table whose source fragments and role evidence were acquired separately. Match complete histories, fragment storage, environment calls and search compute. Compare role-based proposals with literal recipe retrieval and a same-budget composition search. A successful prediction must depend on the acquired substitution relation rather than a hidden source example containing the final composition. Failure on new contexts would refute that extension; it would not erase the observed panel equivalences.

## N3. Grow an experiment library using observed learning value

**Origins and target.** R2-B2-01/10/11/23, with R2-B2-03 as a wider unimplemented extension. B2: obtain useful evidence before an adequate world model can predict which experiment will separate it. The reference operation constructs new **action words and reusable fragments**; it does not receive a list of complete experiments or predicted outcomes.

**State.** The N1 learner with all its state; an acquired recipe library `F`; a macro library `M`; per-recipe observed utility sums/counts; a proposal queue with parent provenance; trial counter `t`; and temporary before/after N1 snapshots plus a validation queue. Configuration additionally fixes validation length `V >= 1` and coverage period `q >= 2`. Warm-up executes each single-action word once in action order, supplying its trace to N1, then puts those words in `F` and `M`. Their utility is initially zero. Shared resource limits count these trials.

Set `t=0` after warm-up and increment it once immediately before reserving each subsequent experiment. Thus coverage applies when `t` is divisible by `q`; the validation-action start index uses that same `t`. Record it even if the reserved experiment later becomes incomplete.

**Construct, execute, then credit.** After warm-up, every `q`th experiment chooses the first primitive word of length at most `L` never executed as a complete trial. Other experiments construct proposals by taking a word in `F` and either inserting a macro from `M` at a primitive boundary, deleting one primitive, replacing one primitive by a macro, or swapping adjacent primitives. Flatten to primitive words, discard empty or overlength results and deduplicate. A child may have several parents. Its proposal score is the maximum parent mean utility, with zero for an uncredited parent; ties prefer the shorter child, its serialization, then parent acquisition index. Choose the highest-scoring child not previously executed as a complete trial. If there is no such child, use the coverage rule. If the finite word set is exhausted, stop rather than fabricate an experiment.

Before executing chosen word `w`, freeze `R_before`, a copy of the current N1 state. Execute `Trial(w)`, feed all completed transitions to the live N1, and freeze `R_after`. Record exactly which predicates were newly selected during this trial. Next issue `V` primitive actions in cyclic supplied action order, beginning at index `t mod |A|`, **without resetting between them**; these are additional, counted validation interactions. Before each validation observation arrives, ask both frozen copies for their next-observation distribution using the actual available history. Do not fit either copy to the validation observations. The live learner may append them only after the whole validation block is scored.

If at least one predicate was newly selected in the trial, set `utility(w)` to the mean over the validation block of `Brier(p_before,y) - Brier(p_after,y)`; otherwise set it to zero. Here `Brier(p,y) = sum_z p(z)^2 - 2p(y) + 1`, computed in exact rational arithmetic from the N1 counts. With `D=|O|`, the squared-probability sum is `(sum_z count(z)^2 + 2n + D)/(n+D)^2`, so a dense enumeration of unseen observations is unnecessary. This fixes score signs and ties without a floating-point tolerance. An incomplete trial or validation block receives no utility credit; keep its actual trace. Add the completed trial word to `F` regardless of utility and update its utility sum/count if available. On strictly positive utility, add every nonempty contiguous fragment of `w` to `M`, with the trial and credit as provenance. On later proposal generation those acquired fragments can be inserted as units. All new words and fragments are literal compositions of the supplied primitive actions.

If either frozen predictor returns a nonnumeric status on any validation transition, mark the credit `UNAVAILABLE` for the entire block. It contributes neither a zero nor a fabricated favorable value to the utility mean, and adds no macros. The actual completed trial can still enter `F`. Complete warm-up and the two count-table snapshots are prerequisites for starting scored experiments; resource exhaustion can therefore stop the operation before any utility is available.

**What changes.** The experiment repertoire and reusable macro vocabulary grow from executed procedures; observed subsequent predictive use changes which descendants are tried. The grammar of mutation operators, validation policy and N1 grammar remain supplied. Counting predicates alone earns no credit. Nevertheless, this utility is a local learning signal, **not proof that a new predicate caused the improvement**: the copies also differ in ordinary observation counts. A later causal comparison must separate feature construction from additional fitting. The validation stream is generated here and is not the eventual test set or a disclosed future goal.

**Dependence, failure and cost.** This is a defined N1–N3 loop: the experiment generates transitions; N1 changes predicates; frozen copies predict later observations; their score updates the recipe generator. No external helper decides which experiment was truly informative. The hypothesis is limited to the usefulness of this local prediction-based credit; it does not anticipate arbitrary future demands. If N1 has no useful grammar, the acquisition signal can be empty or misleading. Cost includes proposal enumeration up to `O(|F||M|L)` generated mutations per selection before deduplication, two full N1 copies, all N1 reconstruction/search, `V` additional actions and predictions, and full retained trial provenance. Exact implementations may share immutable data, but that storage still counts once, not zero times. There is no prescribed laptop-sized setting.

**Later diagnostic contrast.** Hold the primitive interface and N1 learning rule fixed; compare the acquired trace under this generator with primitive-word coverage, the same mutation grammar without learned macro/utility updates, and a matched-compute experiment selector. Then replay the acquired histories through the same downstream learner. A gain must be attributable to newly acquired useful distinctions, not more environment calls, target-test feedback or a hidden adequate world model. If improvement requires credit from the actual held-out future task, this operation's stated claim fails.

## N4. Learn recipes that reopen observable contrasts

**Origins and target.** R2-B1-06/07/08 and R2-B2-05/06/13/17. B2: the learner's own effective action can alter which distinctions it subsequently observes. The learned object is a recipe-conditioned record of lost and restored observable contrasts. It is deliberately weaker than a unique diagnosis of a sensor fault or hidden causal variable.

**State and acquired controls.** Maintain an acquired word library `F`, completed-trial effects, a FIFO queue of contrast experiments, and a restoration catalogue with full provenance. Seed `F` by completing `m` trials of the empty word and then `m` trials of each primitive action, in order; then add every nonempty contiguous fragment of every executed word. A candidate control recipe `c` is any acquired word with a REPEATABLE endpoint different from the REPEATABLE endpoint of the empty word. Here “effective” means a reproducible observable effect; it does **not** claim task reward, protection, communication success or preserved utility. If a task reward later matters, it must be added explicitly to the scientific comparison.

**Crossed experiment construction.** Enumerate newly available tuples `(p,c,q,r,u)` in acquisition/word order: preparation prefix `p`, effective control `c`, two distinct acquired probe words with `q < r`, and candidate restoring word `u`. Include empty `p` and `u`. Require all six following words to fit `L`: `p q`, `p r`, `p c q`, `p c r`, `p c u q`, `p c u r`. Append each tuple once to the FIFO queue. Each call works on the oldest unfinished tuple, executing the first of its six words without `m` completed trials, in the listed order. Identical words share their actual trial entries. Completed tuples leave the queue but retain their data; new acquired fragments create new tuples. If a tuple contains VARIABLE effects, record `VARIABLE_CONTRAST` and do not infer restoration from it.

If the queue is empty, execute one trial of the first primitive word of length at most `L` with fewer than `m` completed trials, acquiring its fragments and reevaluating controls afterward. This covers the case in which primitive actions have no immediately visible effect but a composition does. If every word is covered and no unfinished tuple remains, return `SEARCH_SPACE_EXHAUSTED`; the absence of an effective acquired control is recorded separately. No queue entry is inferred from an unrepeatable empty-word baseline.

**Update.** Once all six effects are repeatable, let their endpoints in that order be `(a,b,cq,cr,uq,ur)`. Record `RESTORED_OBSERVABLE_CONTRAST` precisely when `a != b`, `cq == cr`, and `uq != ur`. Preserve all other patterns under their literal six-endpoint signature. A positive record adds `(p,c,q,r,u,signature,block,trial_ids)` to the catalogue. It does not claim `uq=a` or `ur=b`, and does not silently equate restoring any contrast with restoring the original latent cause. Those stronger equalities, if observed, can be read from the same literal record.

**Reading and use.** Query `(p,c,q,r)` returns all actually supported restorers in increasing `len(u)` then word order, or `UNTESTED/NO_MATCHING_PATTERN/VARIABLE_CONTRAST` as appropriate. It returns the exact conditions and observations, not a hidden-world diagnosis. A caller requesting execution of `u` must show that the issued primitive actions since the last physical RESET are exactly `p c`; otherwise return `NEEDS_PREPARATION`. This is a check on the actual logged action history under the declared deterministic reset contract, not a claim that equal visible states suffice. Executing `u` and any subsequent probe consumes their full actions and changes the world. New preparations do not automatically inherit a restorer.

Reject malformed, overlength or unordered (`q >= r`) queries as `INVALID_QUERY`. Recheck catalogue entries against the current block and effect-table version before returning them. A query response is structured: it contains the list of currently supported restorers, the list with VARIABLE evidence, and a flag for incomplete/untried candidates. Its top-level status is `SUPPORTED` if the first list is nonempty; otherwise `VARIABLE_CONTRAST` if the second is nonempty; otherwise `UNTESTED` if any candidate is incomplete/untried; otherwise `NO_MATCHING_PATTERN`. Historical positive records that fail the current six-cell test remain historical and are absent from the supported list. Mixed positive and unknown cases never imply that every candidate was tested.

Executing a catalogued restorer requires its current-block support check to pass as well as the exact logged preparation check. A matching action word in a different block is insufficient. ## N5. Synthesize readers from lessons about carrier changes

**Origins and target.** R2-B3-01/06/07/09/13, with the retained-constraint extremes kept visible. B3: acquire a reading program from the learner's own experience of state changes rather than receive an analyst's inverse or coordinate formula. This is the lower-priority candidate; it does not solve initial world learning.

**Supplied learning-state interface.** A base learner has a finite numeric state vector `s`, a deterministic ordinary prediction function `Base(s,x)` for a finite numeric query vector `x`, and a deterministic update `Update(s,e)` for a permitted learning record `e`. Their code, vector serialization and query encoding are supplied and charged as part of the base system. An episode produces a pre-change state `s0`, actual subsequent learning records `e1..ek`, and queries the learner can construct from its permitted encounters. No evaluator answer to the target old query enters these functions or the synthesis interface. This specification does not establish that every neural or symbolic system can be exposed through this interface.

In this reference, vector dimensions `d_s,d_x` are fixed in advance and Base returns a **scalar** with a finite rational encoding. Other shapes or nonfinite outputs give `UNSUPPORTED_INTERFACE`, not an implicit vector extension. Query selection has no semantic oracle: configuration fixes `Q_max`, and each source episode uses the first `Q_max` distinct query vectors in that episode's actual ordinary-prediction log **before** its designated `s0` snapshot. Order is first occurrence. An episode with no such query contributes no lessons. Queries are episode-local; they are not pooled or chosen using a later target query.

**Lessons and state.** During designated source acquisition episodes, take permitted copies of `s0`. For each actual subsequent update prefix, compute `s1` on a copy, and for each already available source query `x` form a lesson `(s1,x,Base(s0,x))`. The target is the learner's own old response, not automatically a true world answer. Store source episode provenance, updates, source-query provenance, lessons, learned program fragments and the synthesis search state. Temporary copies and saved old responses are explicitly additional resources. Source episodes are divided by acquisition episode index into an odd-index construction set and an even-index checking set; records from the same episode never enter both. If either set is empty, return `INSUFFICIENT_SOURCE_LESSONS`.

**Program grammar and search.** A reading program takes only `(current_state,current_query)` as runtime data. Leaves are individual state/query components and constants `-1,0,1`; operators are `+`, `-`, `*`, protected division and `IF_LESS(a,b,c,d)`. Protected division returns a typed invalid value when the denominator is zero; invalid values propagate. `IF_LESS` evaluates the comparison and only the chosen branch. Use exact rational arithmetic on serialized rational inputs; a base system using floating point must declare an exact rational encoding of its stored finite values, without claiming the synthesis duplicates floating-point update semantics. The base learner's own output is encoded by the same rule.

Configuration fixes maximum syntax size `S_read`, nonnegative rational lesson-error weight `lambda` and fragment limit `K_read`. Enumerate well-typed trees up to `S_read` in size/serialization order, using every available component index. Evaluate each on the construction lessons. Score is mean squared error plus `lambda * expanded_tree_size`; any invalid output makes its score infinite. Select the minimum score, then smaller expanded size, then serialization order. Evaluate that selected program on the checking lessons and retain the literal errors; checking data do not select another program or change its grammar in this pass. Store up to `K_read` distinct non-leaf subtrees of the selected program, in decreasing occurrence count then size/serialization order, as macros for the next source episode. Macros expand to their original trees and count their full expanded size for the bound/penalty. The returned exhaustive optimum therefore does not change with a cache.

For avoidance of ambiguity, candidate enumeration always uses the canonical expanded-tree order above. The stored subtrees provide memoized evaluation reuse only, keyed by the exact construction-dataset version; they never reorder candidates based on an as-yet-unknown score. Checking-lesson errors never enter fragment selection or a later construction set. Accumulated source lessons retain their original episode partition, and every new synthesis pass records exactly which source episodes it used.

**Reference output and target invocation.** The learned object is the selected reading program and its construction/checking provenance. If no finite-score program exists, output `NO_PROGRAM_IN_SEARCHED_CLASS`; interrupted enumeration outputs `SEARCH_INCOMPLETE` with the actual candidate prefix and no claim of an optimum. At a target discontinuity, freeze the source-trained program before receiving any target old answer. Its only inputs are the current target state and current query. It returns the computed value plus `SOURCE_TRAINED_CONJECTURE`, or `INVALID_READING`; no target validation oracle upgrades the status. The construction/checking lessons and learned program may be retained, but the program cannot inspect their raw contents at target inference. Any further target-world interaction is a separately declared reacquisition variant, not retained-state decoding.

The source/target boundary is stronger than withholding labels: freeze source-episode membership, source-query selection, grammar, synthesis parameters, selected program and checking record **before exposing any target state or query to the synthesis process**. Only the frozen program then receives target `(s,x)`, of the configured shapes. No target-dependent source-lesson selection, cache update, program search or checking-example construction occurs. Holding target relation instances apart from source lessons is a required later data-protocol condition, not something episode parity alone guarantees.

**What is learned and what it costs.** The decoder expression is discovered through source episodes, rather than installed as an evaluator-supplied formula. The grammar and base-system interface remain strong priors. Source targets and the selected program are history-bearing channels; this cannot inherit the earlier observer's restricted memory allowance. With `P_read` candidate expanded trees, `n_read` lessons and maximum size `S_read`, direct evaluation costs `O(P_read n_read S_read)` rational operations, whose bit costs must also be charged; copies, lesson generation, subtree bookkeeping and all retained records add cost. The macro cache is an implementation reuse device in this reference, not a claim of a separate learned search algorithm. Query encoding, stored source answers and the program can themselves contain information relevant to later questions.

**Later diagnostic contrast.** Hold all source lessons, query encodings and compute fixed while replacing the target carrier with a matched carrier that never acquired the target relation. Hold target relation instances out of source lessons, including their update/query provenance; splitting only rows from the same episode would leak. Compare ordinary prediction, source-trained program, an equal-compute synthesis/search control and an explicitly supplied decoder as a diagnostic upper control. Improvement must depend on target-specific information in the changed carrier, rather than answers stored in source lessons or readout constants. Reproducing an old inaccurate response is successful behavioral access but not correct world knowledge. Generalization to unobserved update kinds and larger nonlinear systems remains untested.
