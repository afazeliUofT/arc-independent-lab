## Adversarial cases that must be addressed

1. **An overly broad negative:** finite fresh credit may be an intentional finite discovery burst. Existing useful predicates and parent scores can still produce useful behavior. The assessment must compare the actual limited claim with the criticism, not a stronger invented promise.
2. **An unreachable failure:** a plausible pair of N1 states may never arise through N3's prescribed scheduler. Require complete reset/action/update/validation ordering for a whole-loop history. The existing positive confound remains conditional until that obligation is met.
3. **An information or comparator mismatch:** a history reader, supplied reset, source answer, grammar, or extra validation interaction may explain an apparent advantage. State equal-input and equal-cost requirements for any proposed comparison and distinguish an operational identity from an earlier named whole method.

The strongest immediate check is derivation of the finite-credit bound from the literal append-only and utility-gate rules, with explicit counterarguments. Its PI proof and scope checks are in the new semantics note. This is mathematical review preparation, not a treatment run or self-issued verdict. The independent reviewer must redo the derivation.
